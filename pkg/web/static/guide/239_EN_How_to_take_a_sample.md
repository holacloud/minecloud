# How to take a sample

![Test tube](item:test_tube)

Hold a `test_tube` and **right click water or moss**. The tube becomes a sample and the block stays where it is. You can take as many as you have tubes for.

3 `test_tube` cost 1 `glass` in the C menu.

## What is in it depends on where you are

| You dipped it in... | You get | Tardigrades? | Other life |
|---|---|---|---|
| A lake with mountains close by | ![](item:sample_mountain_lake_a) Mountain Lake Sample | in **half** of the places | amoebas, diatoms |
| A forest lake | ![](item:sample_forest_lake_a) Forest Lake Sample | in about **a third** | rotifers, paramecia, vorticellas, water fleas, diatoms, spirogyra |
| A pool in the desert | ![](item:sample_oasis_a) Oasis Sample | **never** | euglenas, cyanobacteria |
| Any other lake | ![](item:sample_plains_lake_a) Plains Lake Sample | **never** | paramecia, amoebas, water fleas, euglenas, spirogyra, cyanobacteria |
| ![](item:moss) Moss | ![](item:sample_moss_a) Moss Sample | in **more than half** | rotifers, plant cells |
| Water someone poured | ![](item:sample_still_water) Still Water Sample | **never** | a few bacteria, little else |

The rule is easy to remember: **cold or shady water, with moss nearby**. The first lake you find will probably be on the plains, full of life but without a single tardigrade. The good ones are a short walk away.

## The same place, the same contents

- What a tube holds is **fixed when you take it**, and it never changes in your inventory. Nothing spoils.
- Each lake is divided into patches of 8 × 8 blocks. **The same patch always gives the same**, so some parts of a lake have tardigrades and others do not. Found nothing? Don't dip again: walk a bit.
- Each block of moss has its own contents, so try another block.
- Samples are called A, B, C and D, and all those of a lake look the same. **The letter does not say what is inside**, and A in a forest does not mean A in the mountains. You have to look.

## Poured water

Water from a [bucket](111) has come from who knows where. If the water is not where the land itself makes a lake, it counts as poured: above the level of the lakes, or anywhere over dry land. It holds very little.

## Moss

![Moss](item:moss)

It grows on the **shores of forest and mountain lakes**, within a few blocks of the water, in soft dark green patches. You can break it and carry it (a shovel helps), but a sample only counts where you take it: the place decides what is inside. It is a good sign that you are in the right sort of place, though the water holds its own life without it.

Ground you have already explored keeps the shape it had, so moss only grows on land nobody has walked yet.

## Science and game

**True:**

- Different waters do hold different life, and a pond can change a lot from one bank to the other.
- Moss is a wonderful place to look. Its leaves hold on to a film of water, and rotifers, tardigrades and nematode worms live in it.

**Simplified in the game:**

- Real [tardigrades](240) live **almost everywhere**, also in plains and even in deserts. The game puts them in certain places so that finding them is a search.
- A sample in a real tube changes with time: some things die, others multiply. Here it stays as it is, because nothing changes in the inventory.

> **Try it for real, with care:** take a little pond water or some wet moss in a clean jar, and **never drink it**. Wash your hands afterwards. Pond water is harmless to look at, but not to swallow. In the game nothing can hurt you; in the real world some microbes can.
