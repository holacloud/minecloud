# Oven

![Oven](item:oven)

The oven **really cooks**. Right click it to open it. It has three slots, and you only have to fill two:

| Slot | What goes in |
|---|---|
| **Food** | something from the table below |
| **Fuel** | coal, planks or logs |
| **Result** | what is ready: take it out |

When it has food and fuel it lights **by itself**, and when it runs out of fire it goes out by itself: there is no switch. While it burns it gives **light** (level 11), and everyone sees the same flame. The bars in its panel show how much fire is left and how the food is doing, for every player with it open.

It cooks on the server, so you can **walk away** and come back later. It needs no electricity.

| ![](item:mushroom_brown) 1 `mushroom_brown` | ![](item:roast_mushroom) `roast_mushroom` | 15 s |

The **Roast Mushroom** is only made here, and gives **+6** health when eaten (**G**).

## Fuel

| Fuel | Burns for | Cooks about |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:coal_ore) `coal_ore` | **80 s** | 4 fish |
| ![](item:planks) any planks | **30 s** | 1 and a half fish |
| ![](item:wood) any wood log | **20 s** | 1 fish |

Fuel only starts burning when there is food that can be cooked and room for the result. But fire that has been lit burns out, even if you take the food away: you cannot put a fire back in the sack.

If the result slot holds something else, the oven waits until you empty it. If the fire goes out halfway, the food keeps what it had cooked and goes on when you add more fuel.

The [C menu](11) still cooks fish and bakes bread too, with no oven and no waiting. The oven makes you wait, and saves coal: one piece of coal cooks four fish, against one each in the menu.

The oven is for food. Sand, stone and other materials go in the [foundry](235) and the [electric arc furnace](236).

## Why food turns brown

Heat does more than warm food up. When the surface of a piece of food gets hot and dry, above about 140 °C, its proteins and sugars start reacting together. They make hundreds of new substances, brown in colour and full of smell: that is the crust of bread and the golden skin of a roast. It is called the **Maillard reaction**, after the French chemist who described it in 1912.

It cannot happen in a pot of boiling water, which stays at 100 °C. That is why boiled fish stays pale and fried fish does not, and why the inside of a loaf is white, as it is too wet to get that hot.

An oven cooks in three ways at once: hot air touches the food, the walls shine heat onto it as **invisible light** (infrared), and the tray passes heat on by touch. See also the [fridge](234), which does the opposite.
