# Sterilising: autoclave, filter and mould

![Penicillium mould](mob:mould)

To grow a single microbe, a plate has to start **sterile**: with nothing alive on it. If not, what you did not sow grows too. In the [incubator](253) that is mould, and that is not always bad: it is how penicillin was found.

## The autoclave

![Autoclave](item:autoclave)

A steel pot that heats water **under pressure**. Right click to open it, like an oven.

| Put in | Comes out | Time |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:used_dish) `used_dish` | ![](item:petri_dish) a sterile `petri_dish` | 10 s |
| ![](item:culture_mould) a culture plate you are done with | ![](item:petri_dish) a sterile `petri_dish` | 10 s |
| ![](item:agar_plate_unsterile) `agar_plate_unsterile` | ![](item:agar_plate) a sterile `agar_plate` | 15 s |

It draws **40 W**, and only while it has something in it, like the [arc furnace](236). Without power it waits and keeps what it has done. Its water is its own: you do not need to add any.

## Without the autoclave: the plate with mould

You can also pour agar on a dirty dish, not sterilised:

| Recipe (C key) | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| ![](item:agar_plate_unsterile) 1 `agar_plate_unsterile` | 1 `used_dish` + 1 `wheat` + 1 `water_flask` |

Its rim is dull and its tape red, not striped as the autoclave leaves it, and it carries **mould spores**. In the incubator the mould grows by itself, eats the nutrient of what you sowed, and that plate comes out as a ![](item:culture_mould) *Mould Plate* (`culture_mould`). Try it: two plates with the same milk, one sterile and one not, and compare their curves.

Lactobacilli and soil bacteria are also **held back** by the mould. Look at the plate, or press **🔍 Look** in the incubator: around each patch of mould there is a **clear ring**, where the bacteria do not grow. Yeast is not held back, as it is a fungus too.

## Fleming's story

In 1928 **Alexander Fleming** came back from a holiday and found plates of bacteria he had left uncovered. On one, a blue-green mould had grown and, around it, the bacteria had gone. The mould gave off something that killed them. He called it **penicillin**, after the mould, *Penicillium*.

It took more than ten years, and the work of other scientists, to purify it and make it in quantity. It was the first **antibiotic**, and it has saved millions of lives. It all began with a spoilt plate: **a failure that turned out to be a discovery**.

## The water filter

![Water filter](item:water_filter)

A tube of **sand and charcoal**. Use it like a test tube, aiming at water: the filter becomes a `sample_filtered`. Look at it under the [microscope](238) next to an ordinary sample from the same spot: what is big stays in the filter, and only a few bacteria get through.

## Science and game

**True:**

- Boiling is not enough: some bacteria make **spores** that last hours in boiling water. Under pressure water boils hotter, at about **121 °C**, and that does kill spores, in about 15 minutes.
- Fleming's story is real, and the clear ring around the mould is what he saw.
- Penicillin breaks the wall of bacteria. It works best on those whose wall is bare, such as lactobacilli and many soil bacteria. On fungi, such as yeast, it does nothing.
- A sand filter keeps back nearly all protozoa and most bacteria, but not all of them.

**Simplified by the game:**

- The autoclave takes seconds, not half an hour.
- Real mould comes through the air to any open plate, not only to those that were not sterilised.
- **Water filtered like this is not safe to drink.** In the game no microbe harms you, but in real life it has to be boiled or treated.
