# Myotragus

![Myotragus](mob:myotragus)

The **mouse-goat** of Mallorca and Menorca: a goat half a block tall, with short thick legs, **both eyes
facing forward** and **two front teeth that never stopped growing**, like a mouse's. It is an ancient relative
of the [goat](32). It is an [extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has to be brought back to
life.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was a
   Myotragus, you take ![](item:fossil_myotragus) `fossil_myotragus` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived Myotragus: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back.

## Behaviour

- **It walks slowly** near its place: at half the pace of a goat.
- **It does not climb, leap or ram.** A goat jumps onto ledges two blocks high; the Myotragus takes steps of
  one block, like any animal that walks.
- It is a grazer: when it stands still, **it lowers its head to eat**.
- It looks around and flicks its ears.
- It is **peaceful**: it attacks nobody and runs from nobody.

There are **2 coats**: reddish chestnut and greyish brown, both with a pale belly.

## Tips

- **Look at it from the front**, and then look at a goat: a goat has one eye on each side of its head; the
  Myotragus has both in front, as you do.
- `tall_grass` is everywhere: take a good handful and offer it one at a time until it stays with you.
- Put it next to a goat: it fits under the goat's belly.
- In the [Bestiary](5), its sheet and the goat's show the two together under the title **Evolution**.

## The real Myotragus

- **When and where.** It lived only on the islands of **Mallorca** and, later on, **Menorca**. Its ancestors
  walked there more than **5 million years ago**, when the Mediterranean Sea dried up almost completely. Then
  the sea came back, and they were cut off. It vanished about **4300 years ago**.
- **Its name** comes from Greek and means "mouse-goat". It was given in **1909** by the British
  palaeontologist **Dorothea Bate**, who found its bones in a cave of Mallorca and noticed its teeth.
- **Size.** It stood about **50 centimetres** at the shoulder and weighed between about **23 and 32 kilos**:
  like a medium-sized dog. In the game it is more or less its real size.
- **The eyes.** A goat has one eye on each side of its head, to see the wolf coming from anywhere. There were
  no wolves on its islands, and the eyes of the Myotragus faced **forward**, like a monkey's: that is better
  for judging distances.
- **The teeth.** As an adult it had a single front tooth left on each side of its lower jaw: **two in all**,
  which grew all its life as they wore down, like those of mice and rabbits. In a relative of the goats that
  is very rare indeed.
- **The legs.** Short, and with stiff bones in the feet. It walked slowly and most likely **could not run or
  jump**. It did not need to.
- **It grew very slowly.** Its bones have rings, like the trunk of a tree. It took about **12 years** to
  become an adult, and some lived to **27**. At first it was thought to grow as a reptile does, stopping in
  bad times; since then, rings like those have been seen in other animals of its group.
- **What it ate.** The leaves and flowers of shrubs. Above all **Balearic box**, a plant that is poisonous to
  nearly everything else: we know from its droppings, which turned to fossils as well.
- **Living on an island.** On a small island there is little food and, if nothing hunts you, no need to run.
  A big brain and sharp senses use up a lot of energy: the Myotragus had a smaller brain and smaller eyes
  than its relatives. On islands, big animals tend to become small and small ones big: see the
  [Nuralagus](277), its neighbour.
- **Why it died out.** It lasted more than 5 million years, and vanished very soon after the first people
  reached the islands. Most scientists think people were the cause, though nobody knows exactly how.
- **Is it the goat's grandfather?** No: it is a cousin. It belonged to the group of goats and sheep, and its
  DNA, taken from its bones, says its closest living relative is the **takin**, a big animal of the mountains
  of Asia.
- **Nobody knows its colour.** Fossils keep bones and teeth, not hair: the coats of the game are a guess.
