## Wall clock

![](item:wall_clock) `wall_clock`: 2 oak planks, 1 copper block, 1 iron ore. It hangs on a wall and its hands show **the time in the game**: morning starts at 6 o'clock, as the sun comes up. A whole day lasts four minutes, so you can actually watch the big hand move. Look at it and the block panel tells you the exact time too.

| ![](item:vase) | Vase | `vase` | 2 brick, 1 sand, 1 cobalt block |

Clay pots hold water for plants because baked clay is full of tiny holes that let the soil breathe. The blue of the vase is the same [cobalt](163) as blue glass: painted on before the kiln, it comes out deep blue, as on old Chinese porcelain.

## Marble bust, globe and fountain

| | In-game name | Id | Made from |
|---|---|---|---|
| ![](item:statue) | Marble Bust | `statue` | 3 marble |
| ![](item:globe) | Globe | `globe` | 2 oak planks, 1 blue wool, 1 [brass](179) ingot |
| ![](item:fountain) | Fountain | `fountain` | 4 marble, 1 [water bucket](111) |

The **bust** is a head and shoulders on a pedestal; its face looks at you when placed. [Marble](59) is limestone recrystallised by the heat and pressure deep inside the Earth: that is why it is soft to carve and shines when polished.

The **globe** is a blue cube with green and ochre lands, on a wooden stand: in this world of blocks even the Earth is a cube. The real Earth is a sphere, and a globe is the only map that distorts nothing. The oldest one kept, Martin Behaim's (1492), does not have America yet.

The **fountain** is a marble basin with water and a spout with its little bowl in the middle. It is a piece of furniture: its water never spills, and a bucket cannot take it.

## Things on the table

Ten small objects that go **on top of** a table, a desk, a bedside table or a shelf (in the block above it): they tell that somebody lives in that room. None is taller than half a block. Those with a front turn it towards you when placed; the plate, the fruit bowl and the jug look the same from every side.

The **open book** has red covers and writing on both open leaves; the **pile of books** is four of them, blue, red, green and ochre, a little askew. The **letter** is half written, next to a brass inkwell with its black quill, and the **scroll** shows a map with a coast and a route in red. On the **plate** a bite is missing from the bread and another from the cheese. The **tea set** is white porcelain with a band of blue [cobalt](163), like the vase. The **jug** is baked clay, and the **chessboard** has a game left half way, with white and black pieces off their squares. In the **sewing basket** there are balls of coloured yarn and a hoop with a flower embroidered only half way.

Real paper is made of plant fibres, like the wheat of these recipes; the parchment of old scrolls was skin, and paper reached Europe from China through al-Andalus.

Everything here stays where you put it even if the wall behind it goes, as [signs](64) do.
