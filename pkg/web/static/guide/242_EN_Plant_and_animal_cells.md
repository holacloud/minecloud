# Plant and animal cells

![A plant cell](mob:plant_cell)

Every living thing is made of **cells**, and many are only one. You, a sheep and an oak are all vast cities of them. They are too small to see, which is why nobody knew about them until there were microscopes.

| What to put on a slide | At | What you see |

## Spot the difference

![An animal cell](mob:animal_cell)

| | Plant cell | Animal cell |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | boxy, fixed | irregular, soft |
| **Wall** | yes, stiff, of cellulose | none, only a thin membrane |
| **Chloroplasts** (green) | yes: they make food from light | none: it has to eat |
| **Vacuole** | one huge one, full of water, which keeps the cell firm | small ones, if any |
| **Nucleus** | yes, pushed to one side | yes, usually in the middle |

Both have a **nucleus**, which holds the DNA: its [genes](130) are the instructions for the whole living thing.

In the plant cell, watch the green dots near the wall. They **move slowly round the cell**, carried along by the moving cytoplasm. This is real, and it is lovely to see in water weeds.

## Why plants don't need bones

A plant has no skeleton. Each cell is a tiny box with a full water balloon inside it, pressing on the wall. Millions of them together hold the plant up. A plant that wilts is only one whose cells have lost water.

## Science and game

**True:**

- Robert Hooke gave cells their name in 1665. He was looking at cork, and its empty boxes reminded him of little rooms, *cellulae* in Latin.
- Cheek cells are a classic school experiment, and they look like this: flat and irregular, with a clear round nucleus. Your cheek replaces them all the time, so you lose nothing by it.

**Simplified in the game:**

- Most cells have no colour: to see a nucleus well in real life you need a stain, such as methylene blue. Here they are already coloured, to show the parts.
- Real leaf cells come in several shapes, and there are layers of them, one on top of another. The game shows a neat single layer.
- Cells have many more parts, such as the mitochondria that give them energy, far too small to see at this power.
- In the game wheat shows the same cells as a leaf, plus some grains of starch. A real grain of wheat is mostly starch.

> **Try it for real, with an adult:** peel the thin skin from inside an onion layer and look at it with a drop of water. No chloroplasts there, as onions grow in the dark, but the walls are perfect.
