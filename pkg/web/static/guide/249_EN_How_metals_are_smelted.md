# How metals are smelted

![Foundry](item:foundry)

You have your [ore](248). Now to get the metal out. There are two ways: by hand with the C key, which is instant, or in the [foundry](235), which takes time but gives **twice as much**.

## What comes out of each ore

| Ore | In the [foundry](235) | Time | By hand (C key) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ![](item:copper_ore) 1 `copper_ore` | ![](item:copper_block) **2** `copper_block` | 15 s | 1 `copper_ore` + 1 `coal_ore` give 1 |
| ![](item:tin_ore) 1 `tin_ore` | ![](item:element_sn) **2** `element_sn` | 10 s | 1 `tin_ore` + 1 `coal_ore` give 1 |
| ![](item:zinc_ore) 1 `zinc_ore` | ![](item:element_zn) **2** `element_zn` | 18 s | 1 `zinc_ore` + 1 `coal_ore` give 1 |
| ![](item:lead_ore) 1 `lead_ore` | ![](item:element_pb) **2** `element_pb` | 10 s | 1 `lead_ore` + 1 `coal_ore` give 1 |
| ![](item:silver_ore) 1 `silver_ore` | ![](item:element_ag) **2** `element_ag` | 15 s | 1 `silver_ore` + 1 `coal_ore` give 1 |

Iron and gold work differently: in the foundry they give 1 `iron_block` and 1 `gold_block` per ore, and many recipes use the ore just as it is. See [Ores](53).

One piece of coal burns for 80 s in the foundry: enough for several batches of ore. By hand, each ore eats a whole coal. That is why the foundry is worth it as soon as you have the bricks to build it.

The **other recipes are still there**: element blocks can be synthesised and metal blocks refined as always, with no ore at all. See [Element blocks](60) and [Fine stones and metals](59).

## What each metal is for

- `element_sn`, `element_zn`, `element_pb` and `element_ag` are the element blocks that [chemistry](115) works with. Each counts as **one atom** at the [lab bench](117).
- `copper_block` is copper **for building**, the one that wires, generators and motors ask for. See [Copper](165).
- To take what you have smelted to the [lab bench](117), the C key turns 1 `copper_block` into 1 `element_cu`, 1 `iron_block` into 1 `element_fe` and 1 `gold_block` into 1 `element_au`. Only that way: an element block does not turn back into building metal.

## How much heat each one needs

| Metal | Melts at |
|---|---|
| Tin | 232 °C |
| Lead | 327 °C |
| Zinc | 420 °C |
| Silver | 962 °C |
| Gold | 1064 °C |
| Copper | 1085 °C |
| Iron | 1538 °C |

That is why tin and lead go so fast in the foundry.

## Why zinc is so odd

To get a metal out of its ore you heat it with coal, which takes away what is joined to it. Zinc does something curious: it takes more heat to free it (about 1120 °C) than to make it boil (907 °C). So zinc **does not stay in the furnace**: it flies off as a vapour, and has to be caught and cooled to turn back into metal. At Zawar, in India, they were distilling it by the 12th century, thousands of years after the first copper.

## How silver is taken from lead

Silver often comes mixed with lead. Heat that mixture with plenty of air and the lead oxidises into litharge, while the silver, far less reactive, is left alone. At the Greek mines of Laurion they were doing this about 2500 years ago.

## A story of copper

Malachite, the green ore, was mined near Sinai about 6000 years ago, and in the Timna Valley, in Israel, it was smelted for copper for more than three thousand years.

> **Careful in real life.** Smelting metals at home is dangerous: it burns, and lead is toxic. Here you can. There, only with people who know how.
