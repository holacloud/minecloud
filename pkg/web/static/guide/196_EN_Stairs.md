# Stairs

![Stone stairs](item:stone_stairs)

A stair is **a slab with a step on top**: half height at the front and full height at the back. In a row they climb
one block for every block forward, and **you walk up them** without jumping.

## How to get them

They are all made the same way (C key):

| Result | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| **4** stairs | **3** blocks of their material |

Breaking a stair gives the stair back.

## How to place them

- **The high step goes the way you are looking.** Look up the slope you want to build and place it: the stair is
  climbed walking that way, away from where you stood.
- **Upside down:** click a **ceiling** (the underside of a block) or the **upper half** of a wall and the stair
  goes in upside down, hanging from the top. Good for eaves, cornices and under another flight of stairs.
- Like any solid block, it cannot go where you are standing.

## How to use them

- **Walk up them:** each step is half a block and your stride clears up to half a block. The view rises
  smoothly, as on a ramp.
- Animals and monsters climb them too (they already climbed one block).
- They break faster with the tool for their material: an [axe](70) for wooden ones, a [pickaxe](68) for stone.

## Tips

- An upside-down stair on the ceiling facing a normal one on the floor makes an arch.
- Stone stairs need smooth `stone`: mining gives cobblestone, so smelt it first (see [Stone and bedrock](50)).
- Stairs block the sky like a block: there is shade under them.

## Trivia

- The part of a step you tread on is the *tread*; the vertical part is the *riser*. In houses they are about
  28 cm and 18 cm; here each is half a block.
- The spiral staircases of medieval castles usually turn clockwise going up: the defender, higher up, had his
  right hand free for the sword.
