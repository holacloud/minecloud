# How the world is born

Nobody drew the world of MineCloud by hand. Every hill, lake and tree comes from a **formula**: give it some
coordinates and it tells you which block is there. Always the same one.

## Pieces of 16 × 16

The world is split into **chunks**, columns **16 × 16 blocks** wide and **256 blocks tall** (from height −16 to
239). A chunk is only generated when someone comes near, and the ones closest to the player come first.

- **Water** fills everything below height **5**.
- **Bedrock** lies at height **−5** (and sometimes one block higher).
- Under the soil there is **stone** with **ores**: coal at any depth, **iron only below height 4** and **gold
  only below height 1**. See [Ores](53).

## The climate picks the biome

Three smooth "noise maps" are worked out for every column: **temperature**, **moisture** and
**continentalness**.

- High continentalness raises **mountains** (the rocky biome), with sharp ridges.
- Heat and dryness make **desert**, with dunes.
- Moisture makes **forest**.
- Everything else is **plains**.

Biomes have no hard edges: the height is **blended** between them by weight, which is why you climb from the
forest to the mountain up a slope and not a step. See [Biomes](15).

## Lakes, beaches and trees

- **Lakes:** another noise map sinks the ground below the water here and there, **never inside a mountain**.
- **Beaches:** next to the water, at low height, grass turns to **sand**.
- **Trees:** in the forest they grow on 4.5 % of the columns, on the plains only a few, and on the rocks only
  **spruces**. Neighbouring trees tend to be of the **same kind**, which is why you see groves. There are 9
  shapes: round, conical, flat-topped (acacia) and weeping (willow). See [Wood and leaves](54).
- **Cacti** 2 to 4 blocks tall in the desert, and **flowers** that gather in meadows instead of being sprinkled
  at random. See [Flowers and mushrooms](61).

A tree rooted near the edge of a chunk leaves its leaves in the neighbouring chunk. So that both chunks agree,
each one also looks **3 blocks past** its border.

## Dice that always roll the same

The formula uses a **hash**: a sum that mixes the coordinates with a **seed** (a number kept by the server) and
gives back a number between 0 and 1 that looks random but **is not**. The same coordinates always give the
same number. That makes the world **deterministic**: any piece can be worked out on its own, and neighbours
always fit.

## Server and browser, block for block

At first the world was generated by your browser (`terrainGen.js`). On **28 October 2026** the generator was
**translated into Go** and moved to the server, which now builds the chunks and sends them to you.

The translation is **exact, block for block**: a test generates several chunks with both versions and compares
their fingerprints. If a single block changed, the test would fail. The JavaScript version lives on in the
terrain viewer, which draws kilometres of world at a glance.

## What you build stays

The first time a chunk is visited, the server generates it and **saves** it. From then on the saved copy is
the truth: your buildings and holes are kept, and even if the formula changes one day, explored land does not
move. See [Mining and building](10).
