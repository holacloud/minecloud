# Foundry

![Foundry](item:foundry)

The kitchen [oven](233) is for food. Hot work goes in the **foundry**, a furnace of brick and clay that burns **coal**. Right click it to open it. It has three slots, and you only have to fill two:

| Slot | What goes in |
|---|---|
| **To smelt** | something from the table below |
| **Coal** | `coal_ore`, nothing else: wood does not get hot enough |
| **Result** | what is ready: take it out |

It lights **by itself** when it has something to smelt and coal, and goes out by itself. While it burns its mouth glows and it gives **light** (level 13). The bars in its panel show the fire and the work, live, for every player with it open. It works on the server, so you can **walk away**.

## What it makes

| Put in | You get | Time | In the C menu |
|---|---|---|---|
| ![](item:sand) 3 `sand` | ![](item:glass) **4** `glass` | 12 s | 3 `sand` give 2 `glass` |
| ![](item:cobblestone) 1 `cobblestone` | ![](item:stone) **3** `stone` | 10 s | 1 `cobblestone` + 1 `coal_ore` give 2 `stone` |
| ![](item:limestone) 1 `limestone` | ![](item:quicklime) **2** `quicklime` | 15 s | only at the [lab bench](117) |
| ![](item:iron_ore) 1 `iron_ore` | ![](item:iron_block) 1 `iron_block` | 20 s | from `stone` and `sand` |
| ![](item:gold_ore) 1 `gold_ore` | ![](item:gold_block) 1 `gold_block` | 15 s | from `stone` and `sand` |

One piece of coal burns for **80 s**: that is 24 glass, or 24 stone. In the C menu those 24 stone would cost 12 coal.

The **C menu recipes are all still there**, and they are instant. The foundry makes you wait, and gives you **more** from the same materials.

Coal only starts burning when there is a whole batch and room for the whole result. If the fire goes out halfway, the batch keeps what it had done and goes on with more coal.

## How to build it

| Recipe (C key) | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| 1 `foundry` | 8 `brick` + 4 `cobblestone` + 2 `iron_ore` + 1 `coal_ore` |

## Why sand melts into glass

Sand is mostly **silica**, silicon and oxygen stuck together (SiO₂), in tiny hard crystals where every atom has its place. Heat makes the atoms shake, and at about 1700 °C they shake free: the sand runs like honey.

The trick comes when it cools. The melt is so thick that the atoms have no time to line up again, so they freeze **in a jumble**, as they were. A solid with its atoms in disorder is **glass**. Light goes through it because there are no grains or edges inside to scatter it.

Glassmakers add a little soda to melt the sand at a much lower heat. People have known how for more than 3,500 years.

## Why coal, and not wood

Coal is mostly carbon, packed tight, so a piece holds far more heat than a log. With air blown through it, a coal fire gets past 1000 °C. Bricks and clay survive that: they are already fired, which is why furnaces are made of them.
