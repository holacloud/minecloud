# Ammonite

![Ammonite](mob:ammonite)

A ribbed shell coiled into a spiral, with two eyes and a bunch of tentacles looking out of it. It hangs in mid
water and moves **in spurts, shell first**. It died out 66 million years ago, together with the dinosaurs: in
MineCloud you only see one if you find its fossil and bring it back to life.

## What it was

A **cephalopod**: a relative of squid, octopus and cuttlefish. For all its spiral shell, it is **not a snail**.
The animal lived only in the last stretch of the shell, next to the opening; the rest was divided into
**chambers full of gas**, which kept it afloat like a balloon.

## How to get one

1. **Its fossil.** It comes out of common fossil rock, `fossil_rock`, the stone with a coiled shell drawn on it
   (that shell is its very own): it is inside the stone, between y = −11 and y = 2. Breaking it gives you the
   fossil of the animal it held, and that may be a `fossil_ammonite`. See [Fossils](262).
2. **Water.** It is a sea animal: the revival machine has to stand next to **6 blocks of water** joined
   together, or more. A pool that you dig and fill with a [bucket](111) will do, and so will the sea.
3. **The [revival machine](264).** With its fossil inside it takes 20 seconds. The ammonite appears in the water.

## Behaviour

- **It hangs in mid water**, bobbing, with its tentacles open. It spends about a third of its time not going
  anywhere.
- **It moves in spurts**: it squirts water out of the funnel under its head and shoots off **backwards**,
  shell first and tentacles together. Then it coasts, and blows again.
- **It never leaves the water** or goes through a wall, or through the glass of an aquarium.
- **It does not go further** than about 4 blocks from where it was revived.
- **If its water goes away**, it stays on the ground, waiting. It does not die: as soon as the water is back,
  it swims again.

Its shell is about **0.6 blocks** across. There are **3 colours**: cream, white with reddish stripes and
bluish grey.

## The real science

- **When they lived.** The first appeared about **400 million years ago** and the last died **66 million**
  years ago, in the same extinction that ended the dinosaurs.
- **How big they were.** Nearly all of them fitted in a hand, but there were huge ones: the biggest shell known
  was close to **1.8 metres** across, like the wheel of a tractor.
- **How they floated.** A thin tube ran through every chamber of the shell. With it they let liquid in or out,
  little by little, and so went up or down, as a submarine does.
- **How they swam.** By jet, as squid and the nautilus do today: they push water hard out of a funnel and go
  the other way.
- **The tentacles are a mystery.** Soft parts hardly ever turn into fossils, and little but the shell is left
  of the ammonites. Nobody knows how many tentacles they had: probably ten, like a squid. The ones in the game
  are a guess.
- **What they ate.** Very small animals that drifted in the water, and whatever they caught with their
  tentacles.
- **A clock of stone.** The species of ammonites changed very fast, and each one lived for a short time. So when
  a geologist finds an ammonite in a rock, it tells how old that rock is.
- **Their name.** It comes from Ammon, a god of ancient Egypt who was painted with the horns of a ram. To the
  Romans these coiled stones looked like his horns.
- **Why they died out.** 66 million years ago a huge asteroid fell, and the sea changed all at once. Scientists
  think their young, which were tiny, drifted near the surface and were left without food. The nautilus, which
  lays few, big eggs in deep water, survived and is still alive today.
- **Who is left of their family.** They left no descendants. Their closest living relatives are squid, octopus
  and cuttlefish.
