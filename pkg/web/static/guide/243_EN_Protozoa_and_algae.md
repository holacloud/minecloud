# Protozoa and algae

![A paramecium](mob:paramecium)

Plenty of life is a single cell, doing it all alone: moving, hunting or making food, escaping and multiplying, with no nerves, no eyes and no muscles. Look for them in [lake water](239), at **100×**.

**Protozoa** are single cells that act like little animals, as they have to catch their food. **Algae** make their food from light, like plants, and many are single cells too. Neither is really one family: the names are old, and handy.

## The hunters

![Paramecium](mob:paramecium)

**Paramecium.** A slipper covered in thousands of tiny hairs, the **cilia**, that beat in waves and row it along, spinning. When it bumps into something it backs up, turns and tries again: this is about as simple as behaviour gets. Food goes in by a groove in its side. See the two stars that swell and squeeze? They are pumps that bail out the water that keeps seeping in. *In plains and forest lakes.*

![Amoeba](mob:amoeba)

**Amoeba.** No shape at all. It creeps by pushing out **false feet**, and it eats by flowing round its food and closing over it. Those bubbles inside are its meals. *In plains and mountain lakes.*

![Vorticella](mob:vorticella)

**Vorticella.** A bell on a long stalk stuck to something, with a ring of cilia that whirl food into its mouth. Keep watching: every now and then the stalk **springs into a coil**, in a few thousandths of a second, then slowly stretches out. A tiny spring inside it is one of the fastest moving things in any cell. *In forest lakes.*

## The ones that use light

![Euglena](mob:euglena)

**Euglena.** Green like a plant, it swims like an animal, with a whip at the front. A red **eye-spot** helps it find the light. In the dark it can lose its green and live by soaking up food from the water. It puzzled the first naturalists who tried to sort all life into plants and animals. *In plains lakes and oases.*

![Diatom](mob:diatom)

**Diatom.** One cell living in a box of **glass**, two halves fitting like a lid, ruled with lines so fine that they were used to test the best lenses. At **400×** you will see boats and round ones, and at **1000×**, close up, their lines. They make about a **fifth of all the oxygen produced on Earth** each year, and when they die their shells sink and pile up. Some of this rock is mined, and goes into filters and toothpaste. *In mountain and forest lakes.*

![Spirogyra](mob:spirogyra)

**Spirogyra.** The green slime of a pond is threads of it. Each cell holds a green ribbon wound in a **spiral**, with bright beads along it. It is not a single cell but a chain, many centimetres long if you take a whole thread. *In forest and plains lakes.*

## When the slide dries

Cilia and whips cannot row in air. As the [slide dries out](241) they stop and die, though the amoeba and the euglena shut themselves in **cysts**, to wait for better times. Many protozoa really do this, which is why they turn up in any puddle after rain: their cysts blow around in the dust.

## Science and game

**True:** all of these look and act like this: the bump and back-up of the paramecium, the spring of the vorticella, the star-shaped pumps and the shells of glass.

**Simplified in the game:**

- There are many thousands of species of these organisms. The game has one of each, with one way of life.
- In the game they do not eat each other, multiply or divide, except what the [tardigrade](240) eats. On a real slide you may catch a paramecium dividing in two.
- Which lake holds which is the game's decision. Most of them really live in nearly any pond.
