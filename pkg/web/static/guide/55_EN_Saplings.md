# Saplings

![Cherry sapling](item:cherry_sapling)

A sapling is a **tree in miniature**. Plant it and, that very moment, a whole tree of its species appears.

| | Sapling | Id | Tree it gives |
|---|---|---|---|

| ![](item:birch_sapling) | Birch Sapling | `birch_sapling` | birch |
| ![](item:jungle_sapling) | Jungle Sapling | `jungle_sapling` | jungle |
| ![](item:acacia_sapling) | Acacia Sapling | `acacia_sapling` | acacia |
| ![](item:dark_oak_sapling) | Dark Oak Sapling | `dark_oak_sapling` | dark oak |

| ![](item:willow_sapling) | Willow Sapling | `willow_sapling` | willow |

There is no sapling for the common oak.

## How to get them

- **Breaking leaves or grass blocks:** **8 %** of the time you also get a sapling of a **random** species.
- **Crafting it** (C key):

| Result | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| 1 sapling of a species | **2** leaves of that species + 1 `dirt` |

For example, 2 `cherry_leaves` + 1 `dirt` = 1 `cherry_sapling`.

## How to use them

1. Face a **solid block** with the sapling in your hand.
2. Right click on top of it: the space must be empty, or have tall grass or a flower (the tree replaces them).
3. Poof! A tree appears: a trunk **5 blocks** tall (6 for dark oak) with a crown of leaves 5 × 5 below and 3 × 3
   on top. The chat says, for example, "Cherry tree planted".

The sapling is used up. The tree has the same wood and leaves as its species, so you can chop it down right away.

## Tips

- The tree **never replaces your blocks**: it only grows into air, tall grass and flowers. The trunk needs its
  **5 free blocks** (6 for dark oak) straight up, or the sapling is not used and the chat says "Not enough room for
  the tree to grow here". Leaves that would land on a block are simply left out, so next to a wall the crown comes
  out lopsided.
- A **wood farm**: plant, chop with an [axe](70), break the leaves (for more saplings) and plant again.
- Planted trees all have the same round shape; the ones in the world have their own outlines (cones, flat crowns,
  hanging branches).

## Trivia

- A real tree takes **decades** to grow. Some ancient pines in California are nearly 5,000 years old.
- One big tree can cool its surroundings as much as several air conditioners.
