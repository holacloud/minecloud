# Pulmonoscorpius

![Pulmonoscorpius](mob:pulmonoscorpius)

A scorpion **as long as a cat**, with **big eyes** on the sides of its head and a tail that curls high over
its back. It is an ancient relative of the [scorpion](31), and **it is a hunter: it runs at you and stings**.
It is an [extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has to be brought back to life.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was a
   Pulmonoscorpius, you take ![](item:fossil_pulmonoscorpius) `fossil_pulmonoscorpius` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived Pulmonoscorpius: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back.
**Think before you revive one next to your house.**

## Behaviour

- While nobody is near, **it strolls** around its place, opening and closing its pincers.
- **It is hostile, by day and by night.** The scorpion of today waits for the dark; this one does not. When a
  player comes within **10 blocks**, it goes after them: the nearest one, and the same one for everybody who
  is watching.
- **It runs at 1.2 blocks a second.** You walk at 4.5: you can always get away on foot.
- When it is just over a block from you **it lunges its tail** over its head: **2 damage**, at most once every
  1.2 seconds, and **poison**: 4 more points of damage, one every second and a half.
- **It lets go** when you are more than 15 blocks from it, or more than 23 blocks from its home. Then it walks
  back. It never goes further from its home than that.
- If it defeats you, it leaves you alone for 10 seconds.

There are **2 colours**: chestnut brown and dark olive.

## Tips

- **Walk away and it gives up.** It is much slower than you, and it does not leave the surroundings of its home.
- A fence stops it: build its pen **before** you start the machine, with the machine inside.
- Carry `milk` or `antivenom`: its poison does more harm than the sting itself.
- With a [shield](252) in your left hand, and facing it, the sting does half the damage. The poison does the same as ever.
- It always leaves a `venom_sac`, and often two: with `milk`, each one makes an `antivenom`.
- Put it next to a scorpion to see the difference. In the [Bestiary](5), the sheet of both shows them together
  under the title **Evolution**.

## The real Pulmonoscorpius

- **When and where.** It lived about **330 million years ago**, in the Carboniferous, long before the first
  dinosaur. Its fossils come from East Kirkton, a quarry in **Scotland**. Scotland was near the equator then:
  it was hot, with forests of giant ferns, a lake and volcanoes close by. The coal we burn today is what is
  left of forests like those.
- **Its name** means "scorpion with lungs". Its fossils are so well kept that they show its lungs, folded
  like the pages of a book, just as scorpions have them today. That is how we know it breathed air and lived
  on land.
- **Size.** The biggest ones were about **70 centimetres** long. The biggest scorpion alive today is about 20.
  In the game the scorpion of today is drawn far bigger than it is, so that you can see it; that is why the
  two look closer in size here than they really were.
- **Its eyes.** Scorpions of today have tiny eyes and hunt at night, feeling the ground shake under their
  prey. The Pulmonoscorpius had **big eyes** on the sides of its head. Scientists think it hunted **by day**,
  by sight.
- **What it ate.** Probably other small animals: insects, millipedes and maybe some of the first four-legged
  animals, which lived around the same lake.
- **Why so big.** Nobody knows for sure. In the Carboniferous there were also dragonflies with wings as wide as
  a hawk's and millipedes 2 metres long. One idea is that the air had more oxygen than today. Another is that there were
  still few big hunters on land.
- **Why it died out.** Nobody knows exactly. The climate changed, and bigger hunters with backbones took over
  the land. The giant arthropods did not come back.
- **Is it the scorpion's grandfather?** Not quite. Scorpions are older still: the first ones lived more than
  430 million years ago. The Pulmonoscorpius was on a branch that left no descendants: a great-uncle rather
  than a grandfather. And scorpions have hardly changed their shape since.
- **Nobody knows its colour.** Fossils do not keep it. The brown of the game is a guess.
