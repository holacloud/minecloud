# Grand library

![Bookshelf](item:bookshelf)

Behind a portico of columns and a wide flight of steps stands the **grand library**: a stone building of **45 by 60 blocks**, with four storeys of galleries lined with books round a square reading room, and over it a **dome of stained glass** that glows with every colour against the sky. It is inspired by the great domed reading rooms of the 19th century, but it is a design of its own.

## Great libraries of history

People have kept what they know in libraries for almost three thousand years.

| Library | When | What made it special |
|---|---|---|
| **Ashurbanipal's library**, Nineveh (today Iraq) | 7th century BC | Thousands of **clay tablets** written in cuneiform; among them, the *Epic of Gilgamesh*, one of the oldest stories in the world. A fire baked the tablets hard and helped them last |
| **The Library of Alexandria**, Egypt | 3rd century BC | It wanted a copy of *every* book: ships that came into the port had their scrolls copied. Its scrolls were of **papyrus**, a paper made from a reed of the Nile |
| **The House of Wisdom**, Baghdad | 9th century | Scholars translated Greek, Persian and Indian books into Arabic, and wrote new ones on mathematics and astronomy |
| **The library of al-Hakam II**, Córdoba | 10th century | The caliph gathered a huge collection: chroniclers spoke of hundreds of thousands of volumes |
| **The School of Translators**, Toledo | 12th and 13th centuries | Christians, Muslims and Jews worked together translating Arabic books into Latin and Castilian |
| **El Escorial**, near Madrid | 16th century | Philip II's library, with a painted vault. Its books stand with their gilded edges outwards, not their spines |
| **The British Museum Reading Room**, London | 1857 | A round reading room under a great dome, its desks spreading out from the middle like the spokes of a wheel |
| **The Library of Congress**, Washington | founded 1800 | Today the largest library in the world, with well over a hundred million items |

In the Middle Ages each book was **copied by hand**, often by monks, on **parchment** (prepared animal skin). A single Bible could take years. Around 1450 **Gutenberg's printing press** made books quick and cheap to make, and libraries grew as never before.

The **National Library of Spain** was founded in Madrid in 1712.

## A walk through the library

### The way in

A paved plaza, a grid of ![](item:marble) marble on ![](item:limestone) limestone, with benches, flower pots, clipped hedges and lanterns on stone posts. Wide **steps** lead up to the **portico**: thick columns from a stone base to a capital, and over them a triangular **pediment** with, in the middle, an **open book of gold** under a blue lamp. The walls are lined with marble pilasters and tall windows, and the way in is two pairs of doors set in marble, with a band of gold and a tall window of stained glass above them.

### Ground floor

- **The entrance hall**, two storeys high: a floor of marble in a check, a red runner and, on each side of it, a **reflecting pool** of mirrors set flush in the floor, so that you walk on the sky. At the end, a wall of [mirrors](232). Chandeliers, lamps and the **reception** desk.
- **The reading room**, under the dome: long desks along the sides, a long table up the middle and, in the very centre, a **round mirror** in the floor with a gold rim: look down and you see the dome in it.
- **The grand stair**, seven blocks wide, straight on from the door up to the first gallery.
- **The map room**, behind the stair: its floor is a **map** of green land and blue sea, and in the middle a **globe** made of blocks on a gold foot, with white poles. Map tables, candles and paintings of far places.
- **Rooms at the sides**: the **periodicals** (sofas and shelves for newspapers), the **cloakroom** (a row of wardrobes), the **children's corner** (a floor of carpets in rainbow colours, a mural of paintings, sofas, stools and low shelves at a child's height) and a **reading room**.

### The galleries

Round the reading room rise **four storeys of galleries**, each with its walls of ![](item:bookshelf) bookshelves, a marble edge and a railing, so that nobody can fall. Columns go from the ground to the roof. Through openings in the shelves you reach the rooms of each floor:

| Storey | Rooms |
|---|---|
| First | A long **reading room** and the **quiet study**, with desks in a row, each with its chair and lamp, clocks over them and candles by the windows |
| Second | **Archives** on both sides: small rooms, door after door, with chests and barrels; and the **long room** over the entrance hall, with its table and chairs |
| Third | Two more **reading rooms** high up, and another long room with paintings on its walls |

A **spiral stair** in a back corner climbs from the ground floor to the roof, and the **grand stairs** of the upper floors turn in the back of the building. A small archive in the other back corner has chests and barrels on every storey.

### The dome and the roof

The dome is of [stained glass](229): from below you see a **compass rose of knowledge**, a sun in the crown in white, yellow and orange, a **star of eight long points** in red and magenta, a night-blue sky with a few stars between the points, a thin cyan ring and, round the rim, **all 16 colours**. Eight **gold ribs** hold it up. A great chandelier in three tiers hangs under the crown, and a lamp on top shows from far away at night. On the roof, a walk goes round the drum of the dome, with lanterns and skylights.

## Light and colour

- Stained glass lets the sky through: the reading room under the dome is **as bright as outdoors**.
- But no block gives coloured light, so the floor is not tinted: that is why there is a mirror in the middle, to show the colours of the dome.
- The orange and blue lamps along the drum and the galleries are ![](item:neon_lamp) neon and ![](item:argon_lamp) argon lamps: they **glow but give no light**. At night the library is lit by ![](item:candle) candles, ![](item:table_lamp) table lamps, ![](item:floor_lamp) floor lamps, ![](item:lantern) lanterns and ![](item:chandelier) chandeliers: all [lights without electricity](228).

## What the chests hold

The archives' chests keep **scrolls**, piles of books, open books, [blueprints](191), star charts, notebooks and the odd inkwell or globe, never two alike. Their barrels hold what a scribe and a bookbinder use: candles, feathers for quills, coal for the soot of ink, wheat for paper, leather for covers. The cloakroom's wardrobes keep warm coats and wool. There is still room for your own maps and notebooks.

## The science corner

- **Why a dome?** Before electric light, people read by daylight, and candles near paper were a danger of fire. A dome or a skylight brings light into the middle of a big building, where no window reaches.
- **How to find a book among thousands.** In 1876 Melvil Dewey invented a way of numbering books by subject: 500 is the natural sciences, 510 maths, 520 astronomy, 540 chemistry, 560 fossils, 590 animals. Many libraries still use it.
- **Reading a map.** Compare the map on the floor with the globe: a flat map always stretches some part of the Earth, because a ball cannot be flattened without tearing or stretching it.

## Curiosities

- In some medieval libraries books were **chained** to the shelves, because each one was worth a great deal.
- The word *library* comes from the Latin *liber*, "book"; the Spanish *biblioteca* from the Greek *biblion*, "book", and *theke*, "box".
