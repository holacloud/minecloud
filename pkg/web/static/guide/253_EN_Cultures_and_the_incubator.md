# Cultures and the incubator

![Incubator](item:incubator)

Under the [microscope](238) you see a few microbes. On an **agar plate** you can see millions: each spot that grows on it is a **colony**, all the offspring of a single cell. The **incubator** keeps them at the temperature you pick, so that they grow, and shows you how they do it.

**In the game no microbe ever harms you.** Cultures live inside the incubator; nothing in your inventory ever changes.

## Step by step

1. Place the incubator and right click it (on a touch screen, tap it). Its panel opens, with **three plates**.
2. **Put a plate:** pick an `agar_plate` from your inventory.
3. **Inoculate:** pick what to put on it. Each thing carries its own microbes:

| You inoculate | It carries | It gives |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:flower_red) flowers, ![](item:honeycomb) `honeycomb` | ![](mob:yeast) yeast | ![](item:culture_yeast) `culture_yeast` |
| ![](item:milk) `milk`, ![](item:cheese) `cheese` | ![](mob:lactobacillus) lactobacilli | ![](item:culture_lactobacillus) `culture_lactobacillus` |
| ![](item:dirt) `dirt` | ![](mob:soil_bacteria) soil bacteria | ![](item:culture_soil) `culture_soil` |
| a lake sample with cyanobacteria | ![](mob:cyanobacteria) cyanobacteria | ![](item:culture_cyano) `culture_cyano` |
| a culture | what it carries | another culture like it |

   Not every lake sample carries cyanobacteria: if nothing in it grows on agar, the panel says so. Look at the sample under the microscope first.

4. **Pick the temperature** with the slider or with **−** and **+**, from 5 to 45 °C.
5. **Wait**, and watch the [growth curve](254). At the best temperature, in about three minutes the plate goes from a thousand cells to a thousand million.
6. **Take out:** if the plate reached **a million cells** (10⁶, the yellow line of the graph), it gives the culture and a `used_dish`, the dirty dish. If it did not, only the dirty dish. A plate on which nothing happened comes back as it was.

The dirty dish is cleaned in the [autoclave](255): it becomes a sterile `petri_dish` again.

## Power and place

The incubator draws **20 W**, and only **while something grows in it**: empty, or with plates not yet inoculated, it asks for nothing. With power it holds the temperature you set, cooling too (some real incubators do cool).

**Without power** it drifts to the temperature of the place where it stands, the same a [thermometer](112) would read there: warmer by day and in the desert, colder at night, in the mountains and in the rain, and cool and steady in a cave. And the plates grow at that temperature, or they do not.

**Cyanobacteria** make their food from light: they only grow if **direct sunlight** reaches the incubator, by day, or with a lit **grow lamp** near it, as wheat does.

## Science and game

**True:**

- A colony starts from a single cell, and a plate holds thousands of millions.
- Yeast lives on flowers and in honey, lactobacilli in milk and cheese, and a handful of soil is full of bacteria.
- Each microbe has its temperature: yoghurt's lactobacilli like about 42 °C, yeast about 30 °C.
- **Agar** is a jelly that comes from red seaweed. **Fanny Hesse** suggested it in 1881: she used it in her kitchen, and it does not melt in the warmth of an incubator as gelatine does.

**Simplified by the game:**

- Wheat stands for the nutrients. A real plate holds agar and a broth of sugars, proteins and salts.
- Everything is far faster: a real yeast takes an hour and a half to double, and a plate takes a day or two to fill.
- A real culture is picked with a sterile loop, and the plate stays closed. In the game, taking the plate out is enough.
- In a real sample many species would grow at once. The game grows only one of what you inoculate, and the mould of an unsterile plate.
