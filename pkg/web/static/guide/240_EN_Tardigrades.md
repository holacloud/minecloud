# Tardigrades

![Tardigrade](mob:tardigrade)

Eight stubby legs, four hooked claws on each, a see-through segmented body, and a walk like a very small, very slow bear. That is why they are called **water bears**. They are about half a millimetre long, a tiny speck to the naked eye, and they are one of the most amazing animals there are.

## How to find one

1. Build a [microscope](238), and give it light.
2. [Take samples](239) of moss, or of water from lakes in forest or mountains. Plains and desert lakes will not do in the game.
3. Put one on a slide, and start at **40×**: a tardigrade is big enough to see already. Then look at it at **100×**.
4. No tardigrade? That patch has none. Clear the slide and try another place.
5. Click it to identify it. It goes in your [bestiary](5).

To **keep one** you need a [micropipette](238) and an empty `test_tube`: select it and press *Draw it off into a tube*. You get a ![](item:tardigrade_tube) `tardigrade_tube`. It lives only in a tube and under the microscope: you cannot let it out in the world. Put it back on a slide whenever you like.

There is no prize for it. It just goes in your notebook, and you will have seen it.

## What you can watch it do

| It is... | When | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| **Walking** | most of the time | a clumsy walk, its legs moving in waves |
| **Eating** | next to green cells | it stops, pokes the cell with its needles, and its gut slowly turns green |
| **Moulting** | rarely | it stands still, then walks out of its own skin, leaving the eggs inside |
| **A tun** | when the slide dries out | it [pulls in its legs and shrivels into a barrel](241) |

## Its body

- **Legs:** four pairs. The back pair points backwards, for holding on.
- **Mouth:** a tube with two sharp **stylets**, needles that pierce a cell so it can suck out the insides. Behind them is a round muscular pump, the pharynx, which you can see in the head.
- **Gut:** the broad dark sac in the middle. You can tell what it had for lunch by its colour.
- **Round cells** drift inside the body, carrying stored food around. It has no heart and no lungs: it takes in oxygen straight through its skin, from the film of water it lives in.
- **Skin:** the cuticle cannot grow, so it has to shed it. Many tardigrades lay their eggs in the old skin as they leave it, as you may see here.

## Science and game

**True:**

- Tardigrades look and move just like this. There are about 1,300 known species, and new ones are found every year.
- They were first described in 1773, by a German pastor, Johann Goeze, who called them "little water bears". The name *tardigrade* means "slow walker".
- They live **almost everywhere**: moss on a wall or a roof, soil, lakes, the deep sea, the Himalaya and Antarctica. There are probably some within a short walk of your home.
- Some eat plant and algae cells, others bacteria, and some hunt rotifers or even other tardigrades.

**Simplified in the game:**

- They appear only in some lakes and in moss, so that looking for them is a search.
- There is only one kind here, while real species differ. Some have toes in place of claws, and many are not this pale.
- Here eggs appear only with a moult. Real ones can also lay them loose, depending on the species.

> **Try it for real, with an adult:** take a piece of dry moss from a wall, soak it in rainwater in a dish for a few hours, squeeze it out and look at the water at low power. Patience: it may take a while, but this is how tardigrades are really found, by people just like you.
