# The growth curve

![Yeast](mob:yeast)

A bacterium splits in two, and each half splits again: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16... Counting like this soon runs out of room, so the graph of the [incubator](253) uses **powers of ten**: 3 means 10³, a thousand cells; 6 is a million; 9, a thousand million. Each line up the graph is a thousand times more.

## The four phases

Inoculate a plate and watch its curve. The same always happens, in this order:

| Phase | What you see | What is going on |
|---|---|---|
| **Lag** | the line stays flat | The cells have just arrived and **adapt**: they make what they need to feed on what is there. They do not divide yet |
| **Exponential growth** | the line climbs straight | They divide as fast as they can. On a graph of powers of ten, a steady pace is a straight line |
| **Stationary** | flat, at the top | The plate is full: there is no room or food for more. As many are born as die |
| **Death** | it slowly falls | The nutrient is gone. They die, bit by bit |

In the game a full plate lasts about a game day before it starts dying. If you want the culture, take it out in time: it needs at least 10⁶ cells.

## Each microbe, its own temperature

No microbe grows equally well at any temperature. Each has three **cardinal temperatures**: the **minimum**, below which it does not grow; the **optimum**, at which it grows fastest; and the **maximum**, above which it does not grow, and if it is hotter still, it dies.

| Microbe | Minimum | Optimum | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| ![](mob:yeast) Yeast | 5 °C | 30 °C | 42 °C |
| ![](mob:lactobacillus) Lactobacillus | 15 °C | 42 °C | 48 °C |
| ![](mob:soil_bacteria) Soil bacteria | 10 °C | 30 °C | 45 °C |
| ![](mob:cyanobacteria) Cyanobacteria | 15 °C | 28 °C | 38 °C (and light) |
| ![](mob:mould) Penicillium mould | 4 °C | 23 °C | 37 °C |

Look at the shape: the pace rises slowly from the minimum to the optimum, and **drops sharply** soon after it. Too hot is much worse than too cold. That is why a fridge keeps food: in the cold microbes do not die, but they hardly grow.

## The experiment of the three incubators

1. Set three powered incubators to **10**, **30** and **45 °C**.
2. In each, inoculate a plate with the same flower (yeast).
3. Compare the three curves.

At 30 °C the yeast takes off. At 10 °C it takes very long to start and climbs slowly. At 45 °C it does not grow at all, and the line falls: the heat kills it. Then try milk: lactobacilli like 42 °C, and at 10 °C they do not move.

The panel tells you what share of its best pace each plate grows at, at the temperature inside.

## Science and game

**True:**

- The four phases are those of any real culture, and they are drawn like this, in powers of ten.
- The slower the growth, the longer the lag: it is a work of adaptation done at the pace they grow at.
- The shape of the temperature curve is that of a model microbiologists use, the **cardinal temperature model** of Rosso (1993).
- The temperatures in the table are typical of each organism. Every real strain has its own, a few degrees either way.

**Simplified by the game:**

- Time, enormously. The fastest bacteria take about 20 minutes to double, and yeast an hour and a half.
- A real culture is millions of different cells, and the game sums it up in a few numbers per plate.
- A real death phase can last days or weeks, and some soil bacteria make spores that wait for years.
