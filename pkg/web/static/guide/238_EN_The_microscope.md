# The microscope

![Microscope](item:microscope)

A foot, an arm, a stage for the slide, a mirror under it and a brass tube with its lenses. Place it on a table and **right click** it to look. To build against it, crouch.

## It needs light

A microscope shows nothing without light. **With no light the field is pitch black**, and the field itself explains how to light it. There are two ways to light it:

- **The sun**, by day, with the block out in the open: under the sky, under glass, or within two blocks of an opening with nothing in the way. The mirror catches it.
- **A lamp**, day or night: anything that gives light within two blocks: a [candle, a lantern or a lamp](228), a torch, a lit [electric lamp](204) or a lit [light bulb](187). The stronger the light, the brighter the field: a bulb shows the slide, but dimmer than a lamp. A light **in your hand** counts too, within 3 blocks ([the left hand](251)).

An electric lamp or a bulb needs power. If one is beside it and off, the panel says so: give it a generator or a battery touching it, or a wire to one.

If the sun sets while you are looking, the field goes dark. A candle next to it fixes that.

## Preparing a slide

**Select what you want to look at in your inventory or hotbar, then right click the microscope** (on a phone, tap it). If it is free, that uses one `glass_slide`, prepares the slide and opens the panel. With no slides it says so, and how to make them. With anything else in your hand nothing opens: it tells you what to do.

What works:

- A **sample** in a tube ([how to take one](239)). The empty tube comes back.
- **Something you carry:**

| Item | What you will see |
|---|---|
| ![](item:leaves) any leaves, ![](item:wheat) `wheat` | [plant cells](242) |
| ![](item:raw_fish) `raw_fish` | [animal cells](242) |
| ![](item:dirt) `dirt` | [soil bacteria](244) among grains of mineral |
| ![](item:flower_red) flowers, ![](item:honeycomb) `honeycomb` | [yeast](244), and pollen in the honeycomb |
| ![](item:milk) `milk`, ![](item:cheese) `cheese` | [lactobacillus](244) among drops of fat |
| ![](item:mushroom_red) either mushroom | [spores](244) |
| ![](item:white_wool) any wool | scaly fibres, and a few bacteria |

- A **smear of your cheek**: select a `glass_slide` and right click the free microscope. It costs only that slide, and shows [your own cells](242).

The panel only exists with a slide on:

- **Taking it off** (the **Take off** icon) gives it back washed and closes the panel. What was on it is gone.
- **Closing the panel** (Escape or ✕) leaves it on. It goes on drying, and whoever looks next sees it: you with another right click, or another player. That is how the [cryptobiosis](241) experiment works.

If there is already a slide on it, a right click only shows that one and never replaces it, even with a sample in your hand: it belongs to everyone looking. Take it off first. And beware: with a block that works in your hand, such as `dirt` or leaves, the click uses one up. To build against the microscope, crouch.

Everybody looking at the same microscope sees the same slide: the same organisms in the same places. Each one moves in its own way on each screen.

## Looking

Every tool is an icon in the bar along the bottom, with its name under it. They are always the same ones, with none coming and going. If one is grey, press it and it says why, and what you are missing. **Drop** only works once the slide starts to dry, and **Capture** with a tardigrade selected.

| To do this | With a mouse | On a phone |
|---|---|---|
| Move the stage | drag | drag with a finger |
| Change the power | the 40×, 100×, 400× and 1000× buttons, above the focus | the buttons, or pinch |
| Focus | the slider, or the wheel | the slider |
| Identify | click it | tap it |

- **Power.** At 40× you see a wide field and the larger animals. At 400× you see the bacteria, but only a small part of the slide. At 1000× small things are seen close up (a bacterium, a yeast, the nucleus of a cell), the larger animals do not fit and the field is tiny: find what you want at a lower power first. Start low and go up. The line at the bottom of the field is a ruler.
- **Focus.** Everything lies at its own depth in the drop, and what is at another depth looks blurred. The more power, the thinner the part that is sharp, so at 400× you have to keep turning, and at 1000× even more.
- **Identify.** Click what you see. If it is too small for this power or out of focus, the panel says what to do. Otherwise its name and story appear over the field, and it goes in your [bestiary](5).

## Seeing more

- **[Stains](246):** iodine or methylene blue on the slide, with the **Stain** icon. They show starch and nuclei, and they kill what they show.
- **[Filters and dark-field](247):** coloured glass in the light, or a black field where everything shines at its edges, with the **Filter** and **Dark** icons. Neither is used up.

The panel is in the game's language: pick it in this guide or in the settings of the pause menu.

## Science and game

**True:**

- Student microscopes have just these powers: a 10× eyepiece and objectives of 4×, 10× and 40×. Many add a 100× one, which gives the 1000×.
- **1000× wants oil.** That 100× objective is really used with a drop of **immersion oil** between the lens and the cover glass: oil bends light almost as glass does, and lets into the lens what would be lost in the air.
- Mirrors really were how microscopes were lit before electric lamps, and some still are. The light comes up **through** the slide, which is why specimens must be thin.
- More power does mean a thinner slice in focus and a smaller field.
- There is a limit: however good the lenses, light cannot show things much smaller than a quarter of a micrometre. 1000× is already very near it: a bigger image would only be bigger, with no more detail. For a virus you need an electron microscope.

**Simplified in the game:**

- A real microscope turns the image upside down, so the stage seems to move backwards. Here it follows your finger.
- Here 1000× asks for no oil or cover glass, and a button changes the power. On a real one, the oil has to be put on and cleaned off.
- Light in the game has a simple rule, the same at every power. A real microscope needs a lot of light close to it: a candle would be dim at 400×, and dimmer at 1000×.
- Real slides usually have a thin glass cover on top, and stains to make the cells show.

> **Try it for real:** a cheap school microscope, or even a clip-on lens for a phone, shows pond water and onion skin very well.
