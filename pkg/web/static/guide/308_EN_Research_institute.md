# Research institute

![Lab bench](item:lab_bench)

A wide modern building of concrete, pale brick and glass, with a stripe of colour at each floor, **56 by 52 blocks**, three floors high, with an **observatory dome** on the roof: it is a **research institute**, a place where people work at finding out what nobody knows yet. Almost everything in it really works: the microscopes, the lab benches, the ovens, the furnaces, the lift and above all the **electricity**, which runs on the same rules as your own [networks](200).

## What a research institute is

A research institute gathers scientists of several sciences under one roof, with the instruments none of them could afford alone, so that they can share them and talk to each other. A few famous ones:

- **The Royal Institution**, in London (1799). Michael Faraday worked there on electricity and magnetism, and in 1825 he began its **Christmas Lectures** for children, which are still given every year.
- **The Pasteur Institute**, in Paris (1887), founded by Louis Pasteur to study microbes and make vaccines.
- **The Radium Institute**, in Paris (1914), where Marie Curie, the first person to win two Nobel prizes, studied radioactivity.
- **The Junta para Ampliación de Estudios**, in Madrid (1907), presided over by Santiago Ramón y Cajal, the scientist who discovered how the cells of the brain are made. It sent young Spaniards to learn in labs all over the world.
- **CERN**, near Geneva (1954), where thousands of people from many countries study the smallest pieces of matter.

## How a scientist works

1. **Ask a question**: why does wheat grow faster under that lamp?
2. **Guess an answer** that can be tested: a *hypothesis*.
3. **Design an experiment**, changing only one thing at a time and keeping a **control**: the same wheat without the lamp.
4. **Measure and write everything down**, even what went wrong, in a notebook.
5. **Repeat it.** One result can be luck; the same result many times is evidence.
6. **Tell the others**, so that they can try it themselves and find your mistakes. That is why institutes have lecture halls and libraries.

And in a real lab you never eat: food goes in the canteen, never next to the samples.

## A walk through the institute

Each floor has its colour, on its signs and at the back of the lift shaft: **white** the ground floor, **light blue** floor 1, **lime** floor 2 and **orange** the roof. A stair in the middle of the building, two flights in the atrium and a **lift** join them; the lift goes all the way up into the observatory (see [Lifts](218)). Right click a sign to read all of it.

### The plaza

In front there is a paved plaza with birch trees, benches and two pools. In the west pool a ![](item:water_wheel) **water wheel** turns and gives **105 W**; at night a ![](item:daylight_sensor) daylight sensor switches on the **four street lamps**, without any other supply.

### Ground floor

| Room | What there is |
|---|---|
| **Atrium** | A tall hall behind the glass front, with a floor of marble and basalt in a check. On the back wall, the **periodic table**: one block for each element the game has, in its place (see [Periodic table](116)). In the middle, a **double helix** of ![](item:glow_block) glow blocks, like the shape of DNA: only decoration. Reception, sofas, and two flights up to a gallery |
| **Lecture hall** | A stage and six rows of benches that rise to a door on floor 1. On the screen, made of glow blocks: the sun and its planets |
| **Canteen** | A real oven and sink; food in the barrel |
| **Workshop** | Real crafting tables and a **hand crank**: turn it and a bulb glows while you do |
| **Power hall** | Six ![](item:rtg) RTG (480 W), eight batteries, the main switch, and a lever that cuts the RTGs off to leave only sun, wind and batteries |
| **Store and archive** | Shelves, barrels and chests with what a building runs on |

### Floor 1: the living world

- **Microscopes** with real light: those by the window see by the sun, those on the island by lamps, the one at the back by a candle. At night the window ones go black. See [The microscope](238).
- **The darkroom**: go in, shut the door, and the microscope shows black; a lever lights its lamp.
- **The sample room**: real water and real moss, to take a sample with a test tube ([How to take a sample](239)).
- **Genetics**: wheat at its four ages under a ![](item:grow_lamp) grow lamp, which makes it grow twice as fast, and signs about the genes of animals ([Genetics](130)).
- **A small library**, all shelves and silence: the real book is this guide.

### Floor 2: experiments and chemistry

- **The experiment room**: step on the pressure plate and a motor opens the iron door. Inside, benches of [logic gates](209) and [sensors](208), each with its lamp, and a **bench made to black out**: three lamps and a motor use exactly the 80 W of one RTG, and one lamp more switches *everything* off. A network is all or nothing.
- **Three offices**, for a geologist, an astronomer and a biologist.
- **Chemistry**: real ![](item:lab_bench) lab benches with their 30 reactions ([Lab bench](117)), ![](item:fridge) fridges and a freezer, an ![](item:incubator) incubator and an ![](item:autoclave) autoclave for cultures ([Cultures and the incubator](253)), and at the back the **hot bay**: an [oven](233), a [foundry](235) and an ![](item:arc_furnace) [electric arc furnace](236) that makes steel.

### The roof

- **The observatory**: a dome with a **slit open to the sky**. The big telescope in it is a model made of blocks; the real [telescopes](133) are items, in the chest, and you raise them with a right click.
- **The star deck**: open sky all round, to look at the [constellations](132).
- A real ![](item:weather_station) **weather station**, in the open ([Weather station](112)).
- **24** ![](item:solar_panel) **solar panels** and **2** ![](item:wind_turbine) **wind turbines** for the main network.
- **The greenhouse**: real wheat under grow lamps, with a lever on its RTGs.

## What the chests hold

Each room's chests hold what that room is for, enough to work with:

| Where | What you tend to find |
|---|---|
| Store and archive | Coal, torches, candles, sand, glass, buckets, planks, cobblestone, blueprints, measuring tapes |
| Canteen | Bread, cooked fish, cheese, honey, roast mushrooms, wheat, coal for the oven |
| Workshop and power hall | Copper wire, wire posts, bulbs, lamps, batteries, solar cells, a multimeter, levers, buttons, logic gates and sensors, iron and copper blocks |
| Microscopes and samples | Test tubes, glass slides, pipettes, stains, dark-field stops, water samples, agar plates, Petri dishes |
| Experiment room | Logic gates, sensors, levers, a battery block and a multimeter |
| Offices | Rocks (basalt, granite, marble, obsidian, pyrite, fluorite), a telescope and star charts, a breeding notebook, a bug net and an ant farm |
| Chemistry | Element blocks (hydrogen, oxygen, sodium, chlorine, iron, carbon, copper, tin...), flasks, baking soda, copper sulfate, quicklime, salt; cold drinks, ice and agar plates in the fridges |
| Hot bay | Coal in the furnaces, and sand, ores and limestone by the door |
| Roof | Telescopes, star charts, a thermometer, a barometer, an anemometer; seeds, fertiliser and a hoe in the greenhouse |

The ovens and furnaces have only their fuel, so nothing lights until somebody brings food or ore.

## The science corner

The electricity of the institute is the best lesson in the building: it is split into **ten separate networks**, because a network that cannot feed all its consumers switches them all off, and stops working over 512 blocks. [A laboratory that works](250) explains each network and gives things to try.

## Curiosities

- The helix of the atrium has two coloured strands joined by rungs, like the two chains of DNA, which were described in 1953 thanks to an X-ray photograph taken by Rosalind Franklin.
- A telescope wants the darkest, highest place: that is why observatories are on roofs and mountains, and why domes open with a slit.
