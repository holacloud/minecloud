# Apartment block

![Lift stop](item:lift_stop)

A tall modern building, **24 blocks wide and 18 deep**, with balconies on both sides and a garden on the roof: an **apartment block**. Over the lobby there are **11 floors of homes**, 31 homes in all, joined by stairs and by a **lift that really works**.

## Living one on top of another

Building upwards puts many families on a small piece of land, close to shops, schools and work.

- **Ancient Rome** already had blocks of flats, the *insulae*, of up to six or seven floors, so tall and badly built that the emperor Augustus limited their height to about 20 metres.
- In Spain, the **corralas** of Madrid were blocks of small homes round a courtyard, with long open corridors where neighbours met.
- The tall buildings of today were only possible thanks to two inventions of the 19th century: **steel frames** and the **safe lift**. In 1854 Elisha Otis stood on a lift platform in front of a crowd and had its rope cut: a brake stopped the fall at once. The first passenger lift with his brake was put into a shop in New York in 1857.

## A walk through the building

### The lobby

Through a glass front with a pair of double doors you enter a hall with a floor of ![](item:marble) marble and basalt. There are sofas and plants by the window, a desk for the concierge, a clock, a chandelier and, on the side wall, shelves for the **mailboxes**. A sign welcomes you: *Bienvenido a casa*, "welcome home". Behind the lobby there is a **common room** for the neighbours, with a long table and chairs, green sofas and bookshelves.

### Getting up

- **The stairs** go up at the back, half a turn per floor, to the roof.
- **The lift** goes from the lobby to **floor 11**. Over each lift door there is a ![](item:lift_stop) **lift stop**, which is also the call button: **dark** while the lift is free, **amber** while it is called, **green** when it is here. The iron door under it opens by itself when the lift arrives and shuts when it leaves, so nobody can walk into an empty shaft.
- Inside the lift, a **green** button sends it to the top and a **red** one down to the lobby; on the way, click the stop of your floor as it goes by. **The last call wins**: the lift goes straight there.

Each floor has its colour on the carpet of its corridor, on its sign and at the back of the shaft: white for the lobby, then yellow, orange, red, pink, magenta, purple, blue, light blue, cyan, lime and green for floor 11. So you always know where you are.

### The homes

On each floor there are two homes at the front and one at the back; floors **5** and **11** have one **big home** at the front instead. No two are quite the same: there are seven styles, each with its own colours of sofa, carpet, curtains, floor, walls and bathroom, and the kitchen, the bath and the bed change place from one home to the next.

| Room | What it has |
|---|---|
| **Living room and kitchen** | A sofa, a ![](item:fridge) fridge, an ![](item:oven) oven and a ![](item:sink) sink, curtains, a painting or a clock, and in some a bookshelf or chairs. The big homes have a table for six, a desk and more sofa |
| **Balcony** | Its door, a railing, a flower pot, a chair or a bench, and a light |
| **Bathroom** | A ![](item:toilet) toilet, a ![](item:bathtub) bathtub and a sink with a ![](item:wall_mirror) mirror over it |
| **Bedroom** | A ![](item:bed) bed, a ![](item:wardrobe) wardrobe and a bedside table with a lamp |

### The roof

At the top of the stairs a door opens onto the **roof terrace**, behind a railing: on one side a wooden **deck** with benches facing the view, a table, lamps and flower pots; on the other a **garden** of grass with two small birch trees, flowers and a slate path to a bench. Above the core of the building is the **machine room**, with a window to look at the winch that pulls the lift.

## What the chests hold

Every home is stocked, and no two alike. The fridge holds milk and some of cheese, cold water, soda, bread, honey, fish or roast mushrooms, with ice and frozen fish or bread in its freezer; the fridges are not connected, so they work as plain cupboards, but nothing in them needs the cold to keep. Each wardrobe has a warm coat and some wool, leather, a sewing basket, candles or a tool: an axe, shears, a fishing rod, a bug net. The ovens are unlit.

## The science corner

- **Why the lift stops at floor 11.** The lift is a platform pulled up by a **winch** with its own RTG generator (60 of its 80 W). A winch can hold **48 blocks** of rope at most and stops 2 below itself, so the platform travels 46 blocks at the most. The floors are 4 blocks apart: 11 floors are 44 blocks, 12 would be 48. So the last flight, to the roof, is by stairs. See [Lifts](218).
- **Saving land.** Count it: 31 homes stand on the land of three or four small houses. The same land with houses one beside the other would need a whole street.
- **The stops are logic.** The stops, the doors and the buttons use no power at all: they are signals, like the [logic gates](209).

## Curiosities

- In Spain the ground floor is the *planta baja* and the floor above is the *primero*; in the United States the ground floor is already the first floor.
- With balconies on both sides of the building, every home, at the front or at the back, has its own balcony and its own view.
