# Stains

![Methylene blue](item:methylene_blue)

Most of what lives on a slide is nearly as clear as the water around it. A **stain** is a dye that sticks to some parts of a cell and not to others, so that you can see what was there all along. It shows you things no lens could. And in this game, as with most stains, **it costs the life of what you are looking at.**

## Using one

1. Put a slide on [the microscope](238).
2. Press the name of a stain in the panel. It only asks, and tells you what it will cost.
3. Press **Stain it** to go ahead, or *Not this slide* to change your mind.

The bottle is used up and you get its `glass` back. The stain stays with the slide, and **everybody looking at that microscope sees it**. There is **one stain to a slide**. Take the slide off and it comes back washed, with no stain.

## The price

A stained slide is **fixed**, and the panel says so before you stain it:

- Everything on it stops moving, for good.
- It no longer dries: it stays as the stain found it.
- **Nothing can be drawn off it.** The pipette will not take a tardigrade from it.
- **A drop of water brings nothing back.** A [tun](241) on a stained slide stays a tun.

So take two slides from the same sample: one to watch alive, one to stain.

What lies around them counts too: on a `wheat` slide, iodine turns the **loose grains of starch** blue-black. Drops of fat and grains of mineral take nothing.

## Three things to try

- **Is it starch?** Iodine on `wheat`, then on a sample with spirogyra, then on one with euglena. Green alga and green swimmer both make food from light. Only one of them stores it as starch.
- **Your own DNA is in there.** A smear of your cheek with methylene blue, at 400×.
- **The cost.** A slide with a tardigrade: stain it, and then try to add a drop.

## Science and game

**True:**

- Iodine turns starch blue-black because it fits inside the coiled chains of the starch. Its own colour is a light orange-brown, which is what the rest of the cell takes on.
- Glycogen, the store of food of animals, fungi and bacteria, gives a browner, weaker colour with iodine: red-brown, not blue-black. Brewers use this to see how much yeast has stored.
- Spirogyra keeps its starch around its pyrenoids, and iodine is how you find them.
- Euglena stores paramylon, which looks like starch but is built differently, and does not stain with iodine. Diatoms store their food in other forms too.
- Methylene blue has a positive charge, so it sticks to the negative charge of DNA and RNA. The nucleus holds the most, and goes darkest. That is why it is the usual stain for cheek cells.
- Bacteria have no nucleus. Their spores have a wall so thick that ordinary dyes do not get in, and they show as clear gaps. Their whips are too thin to see with an ordinary stain.
- Heinrich Caro first made methylene blue in 1876. Its formula is C₁₆H₁₈ClN₃S.

**Not always fatal.** This is the game's rule, not nature's:

- There are **vital stains**, used in very low doses on living cells: neutral red and Janus green B on protozoa, and even a very dilute iodine. Paul Ehrlich stained living nerve fibres with methylene blue in 1887.
- Living yeast turns methylene blue colourless, and only the dead cells stay blue. Brewers count them that way.
- Researchers have also stained living tardigrades, with fluorescent dyes, and checked that they survive.
- Still, nearly every stain is poison in the end, even the vital ones. It is a question of dose and time.

**Simplified in the game:**

- Here every stain kills at once, whatever the dose, and yeast is always blue. Real strong iodine does kill and preserve: Lugol's solution, iodine in water, is what is used to keep samples of plankton.
- The real stain for the microscope is usually that Lugol's solution. A tincture is iodine in alcohol. The game has one bottle for both.
- A real wet slide still dries after staining. Keeping it for good takes more steps.
- The recipe of methylene blue only stands in for the real thing, which is made in a chemical works from dimethylaniline and a sulfur compound. Do not look for a formula in coal, sulfur and water.
- Which colour each animal takes, and how strongly, is our drawing, not a measurement. We found no source on tardigrades or water fleas with these two stains.

> **Try it for real, with an adult:** iodine solution from a pharmacy on a thin slice of potato shows the starch grains beautifully. Iodine and methylene blue stain skin and clothes, and neither is for drinking.
