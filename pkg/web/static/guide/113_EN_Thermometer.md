# Thermometer

![Thermometer](item:thermometer)

A glass tube with a column of **mercury** inside: when the air warms up, the mercury expands and climbs up the
tube. It tells you the temperature wherever you are.

| | In-game name | Id |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:thermometer) | Thermometer | `thermometer` |

## How to get it

| Recipe (C key) | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| 1 `thermometer` | 2 `glass` + 1 `element_hg` |

Glass is smelted from sand ([Sand, cactus and glass](51)); the mercury block (`element_hg`) is synthesized like
every [element block](60).

## How to use it

- **Hold it**: the panel in the bottom right corner shows the temperature in °C (and °F), a word for it (Freezing,
  Cold, Cool, Mild, Warm, Hot, Scorching) and whether it is **warming** or **cooling** over the next hour.
- **Right click** with it to write the reading in the chat.

## What makes it warmer or colder

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Biome** | Desert: 36 °C in the afternoon, 6 °C before dawn. Plains: 23 / 11. Forest: 20 / 12 (the trees soften the swing). Rocks: 15 / 4. |
| **Time of day** | Warmest around **15:00**, coldest around **03:00**. |
| **Altitude** | Above y = 14, **0.4 °C less per block**: mountain tops can be below freezing at night. |
| **Rain** | It cools the air by 3 to 6 °C (more in the desert). |
| **Under water** | Water barely follows the day: it stays near the day's average. |
| **In a cave** | A steady **12 °C** day and night, a little warmer the deeper you go below y = 0. |
| **Under a roof** | The temperature moves a little towards a comfortable 18 °C. |

Near biome borders it reads a mix of both.

## Tips

- **Desert nights are cold**: the desert has the biggest swing between day and night of all biomes.
- On a hot desert afternoon, a **cave** is the coolest place around.
- The [weather station](112) carries a thermometer inside its white box.

## Trivia

- Mercury is the only metal that is **liquid** at room temperature. It freezes at −39 °C.
- Real thermometers are kept in the **shade**: in the sun they would measure the sunlight, not the air.
- In the real world the air cools about **6.5 °C every 1000 m** you climb. The game exaggerates it so you notice
  it on a mountain.
- The Fahrenheit scale puts water freezing at 32 °F and boiling at 212 °F; Celsius, at 0 °C and 100 °C.
