# Amber

![Amber rock](item:amber_rock)

Under the forests, inside the stone, there are honey-coloured drops that glow a little in the dark. It is **amber**: the resin of trees from millions of years ago, gone as hard as a stone. Now and then it has an insect inside.

## In the game

| | Id | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:amber_rock) | `amber_rock` | stone with drops of amber: the block you find |
| ![](item:amber) | `amber` | a piece of amber with nothing inside |

- **Where:** only under the **forest**, because amber was the resin of its trees. There is none under plains, the desert or the mountains.
- **Height:** in the stone, from y = 3 to y = 12. That is **higher than any fossil**: [fossils](262) end at y = 2.
- Nearly all of it is between y = 3 and y = 5. Forest ground is low, and higher up there is usually dirt already.
- It always has at least **one block of stone over it**, between it and the dirt.
- **How much:** it comes in pockets of 1 to 3 blocks. There is amber in about 2 forest chunks out of 5.
- It **glows** a little, so it shows in a dark tunnel. It gives no light to what is around it.

A piece of the world you explored before stays as it was, with no amber. It only appears in land **nobody has visited yet**.

## What you take with you

- Breaking the block gives you the **amber with the insect** that was inside. That amber is the insect's fossil.
- Which insect it is depends on the exact place of the block, as with [fossils](262).
- If the game has no amber animal yet, the block gives a plain piece of `amber`. It is pretty, and for now no recipe uses it.
- It is rock: a [pickaxe](68) breaks it quicker.

## How to search for it

1. Look for a forest with hills. The higher the ground, the more stone there is between y = 3 and y = 12.
2. Dig down to y = 4.
3. Open a straight tunnel under the forest and watch the walls. When you see an orange glow, break the blocks that touch it too.

## Real amber

- **It is resin, not sap.** Some trees give off sticky resin when they are wounded. It works as a plaster, and it traps the bugs that attack them.
- **It hardens very slowly.** Buried and without air for millions of years, resin turns into amber. Baltic amber, the best known, is about 44 million years old.
- **It keeps insects whole.** An insect that gets stuck ends up covered in resin. Millions of years later it is still there, with its legs, its wings and even its hairs. In rock, fossils are squashed flat; in amber they are not.
- **Not only insects.** Spiders, feathers and flowers have been found, and in 2016 the tip of the tail of a small dinosaur, feathers and all.
- **It is very light.** It floats in very salty water, which is why the sea leaves it on beaches after storms.

> **Can you get a dinosaur out of a mosquito in amber?** In films, yes. In real life, no: DNA breaks down with time. The oldest that has been read is about 2 million years old, and the dinosaurs died out 66 million years ago. Here, in the game, we imagine it.

> **The word "electricity" comes from amber.** Rub a piece of amber with wool and it pulls fluff and feathers towards it. The Greeks called it *elektron*, and that name gave us the word [electricity](200).

The insect in a piece of amber comes back to life in the [revival machine](264), like the animal of any fossil.
