# Smilodon

![Smilodon](mob:smilodon)

The **sabre-toothed cat**: as heavy as a tiger, built like a bear, with **two canines as long as kitchen
knives** hanging below its chin. It is an ancient relative of the [cat](25), and **it is a hunter: it comes
for you, and it hits hard**. It is an [extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has to be brought
back to life.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was a
   Smilodon, you take ![](item:fossil_smilodon) `fossil_smilodon` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived Smilodon: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back. **Think
before you revive one next to your house.**

## Behaviour

- While nobody is near, **it strolls slowly** around its place and looks about.
- **It is hostile, by day and by night.** It does not come from far, as an animal that lies in wait: only
  when a player is within **12 blocks**. Then it goes after them: the nearest one, and the same one for
  everybody who is watching.
- **It runs at 2 blocks a second**, a little slower than the [dire wolf](271). You walk at 4.5: you can always
  get away on foot.
- When it is just over a block from you **it strikes: 5 damage**, at most once every 1.2 seconds. You have 20
  health: that is 4 blows.
- **It lets go** when you are more than 18 blocks from it, or more than 26 blocks from its home. Then it walks
  back. It never goes further from its home than that.
- If it defeats you, it leaves you alone for 10 seconds.

There is one colour only: tawny, darker along the back with a few faint spots, and pale under the belly.

## Tips

- **Do not let it reach you.** Four blows are all it needs: it hits harder than a [cave monster](21).
- **Walk away and it gives up.** It is slower than you, and it does not leave the surroundings of its home.
- A fence stops it: build its pen **before** you start the machine, with the machine inside.
- With a [shield](252) in your left hand, and facing it, its blow does half the damage.
- Put it next to a cat to see the difference. In the [Bestiary](5), the sheet of both shows them together
  under the title **Evolution**.

## The real Smilodon

- **When and where.** It lived in **North and South America** from about **2.5 million to 10,000 years
  ago**.
- **Its name** comes from Greek and means "knife tooth".
- **Size.** There were three species. The best known, *Smilodon fatalis*, stood about **1 metre** at the
  shoulder and weighed between **160 and 280 kilos**, like a big tiger. The biggest, *Smilodon populator* of
  South America, could weigh **400 kilos**.
- **The sabres.** Its upper canines were up to **28 centimetres** long, root and all, flat and with an edge
  like a blade. But they were fragile: they could break against a bone. So it did not bite as a lion does. It
  **held its prey down** with its enormous front legs, and only then gave one deep bite to the throat.
- **A huge yawn.** To use such teeth it opened its mouth about **twice as wide** as a lion can.
- **What it ate.** Big, slow animals: bison, camels, giant sloths and young mammoths. It was built to
  **ambush**, not to chase: short legs, a short tail and a very heavy body.
- **A trap of tar.** In the tar pits of La Brea, in Los Angeles, the bones of more than **2,000** of them have
  been found, next to those of thousands of dire wolves.
- **Why it died out.** When the Ice Age ended, most of the big animals it hunted disappeared, and it could
  not live on small, fast prey.
- **Is it a tiger?** No, although many people call it the "sabre-toothed tiger". It belonged to the family of
  cats, but to a branch of its own that left no descendants: it is neither the grandfather of the tiger nor
  that of your cat, but a distant cousin of both.
