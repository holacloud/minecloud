# Light bulb

![Light bulb](item:light_bulb)

A small electric light: a **tungsten filament** inside glass filled with argon. Give it power and it glows yellow. Without power it is dull glass with a dark coil.

## How to get it

At the [lab bench](117), with a reaction you have to **discover**: it shows as "???" in the list until you mix the right atoms in the flask. Hint:

> *A filament of the hardest metal to melt, in glass full of a lazy gas, gives warm light.*

## What it is for

- Place it like any block. Breaking: **0.2 s**.
- It is a [consumer](204) of **5 W**: it needs a generator or a battery touching it, or a [copper wire](202) to one. It joins networks like any electric block, and the current passes through it.
- With power it gives **light level 7**, about half of an [electric lamp](204)'s 14, and it uses half as much power. It stays lit day and night while its network has power.
- It is enough to light a [microscope](238) within two blocks, though dimmer than a lamp or the sun.
- With glass and copper wire it becomes two [electric lamps](204).
- If you placed bulbs before they took power, they work as they are: the server learns of each one as soon as somebody comes near it. They only go dark until you give them current.

## How a filament bulb works

Electricity has trouble getting through the very thin coil of **tungsten**, and that heats it, to about **2500 °C**: hot enough to glow white. Tungsten is used since no other metal stands so much heat: it melts at 3422 °C.

In air the filament would burn up in moments, as hot tungsten and oxygen react at once, so there is no air inside. And even so, the hot metal slowly boils away, atom by atom, leaving a dark smudge on the glass. A gas that reacts with nothing, [argon](156), presses on it and **slows that down**, so that the bulb lasts for many hundreds of hours. In the end the thinnest point of the coil gives way, and the bulb blows.

## Trivia

- Edison showed his carbon-filament bulb in 1879; tungsten improved it some years later.
- Only about 5 % of the electricity of a filament bulb becomes light: the rest is heat, which is why they were too hot to touch.
- Today almost every bulb is an LED, which gives the same light with far less energy.
- A real 5 W filament bulb is only a night light; those that lit a room took 40 to 100 W. In the game, numbers are scaled for play.
