# Haastʼs eagle

![Haastʼs eagle](mob:haast_eagle)

**The biggest eagle there has ever been**: heavy, with short broad wings, legs as thick as your wrist and
talons like a tiger's claws. It is an ancient relative of the [eagle](33). It circles over its home, and
**it dives at whoever walks under it**. It is an [extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has
to be brought back to life.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was a
   Haastʼs eagle, you take ![](item:fossil_haast_eagle) `fossil_haast_eagle` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived Haastʼs eagle: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back.
It appears on the ground next to the machine, and a few seconds later it takes off. **Think it over before
you revive one next to your house.**

## Behaviour

- **It circles over its home**, about **8 blocks** over the ground, gliding with its wings held out. Every
  4 to 9 seconds it beats them a few times. It leans into its turns.
- **It never goes more than 12 blocks from its home**, the place where it came back to life.
- **It comes down to perch.** After 35 to 70 seconds in the air it glides down to the highest place it sees
  near its home (a treetop, a roof, or the ground), folds its wings and sits there for 10 to 22 seconds,
  looking about. Then it rises **2 blocks** straight up, spreads its wings and goes back to its circles.
- **It never flies into anything.** It flies over what is low and round what is high.
- **With a roof over it, it does not fly.** On the ground it flutters out from under the roof, if there is
  open sky within 4 blocks; if not, it stays where it is. A roof put over it while it flies brings it down,
  slowly, after 4 seconds.
- **It is hostile.** When it is circling and you are on the ground under it, within **10 blocks** of it and
  near its home, **it screeches** (the chat says so too, no more than once a minute). 1.6 seconds later **it dives** at you at **9 blocks
  a second**. If its talons reach you: **2 damage**. Then it climbs away, and it does not dive again for 25 to
  40 seconds.
- **It dives at where you are, not at where you will be.** If you step aside in time, it misses.
- **It never dives at you if you have a roof**: any block up to 5 blocks over your head, when it cries or
  when it is about to dive. Nor if you are in the water, or somewhere it cannot see you.
- Each player sees it fly its own way round the same home, and it dives at each player on that player's own
  screen: the others do not see that dive.

There is one colour only: dark brown, with a golden nape, a pale streaked breast and a grey beak.

## Tips

- **When you hear it screech, move.** You have a second and a half to get under a roof or a tree: then it
  does not dive. If it is already coming down, run to one side.
- **A porch is enough**: one block over your head and it leaves you alone.
- With a [shield](252) in your left hand, and facing it, its talons do half the damage.
- **To hit it, wait for it to perch.** It comes down every minute or so, often on to the ground.
- **Revive it out of doors.** Indoors it cannot take off, and it stays on the floor.
- To keep one as a neighbour, give your paths a roof, or keep 12 blocks away from its home.
- In the [Bestiary](5), its sheet and the eagle's show the two together under the title **Evolution**.

## The real Haastʼs eagle

- **When and where.** It lived in the forests of the **South Island of New Zealand** until about **600 years
  ago**.
- **Its name.** It is named after Julius von Haast, the scientist who first described it, in the 1870s, from
  bones found in a swamp.
- **Size.** The females, which were the bigger, weighed from **10 to 15 kilos**: the heaviest eagles of
  today weigh up to 9. Its wings measured from **2.5 to 3 metres** from tip to tip, which is short for such a
  weight: wings for turning among trees, not for soaring over open land. In the game they measure 2.6 blocks.
- **Its talons.** The biggest were as long as **9 centimetres**, like the claws of a tiger.
- **What it ate.** **Moa**: giant birds of New Zealand that could not fly, some of 200 kilos, more than ten
  times its own weight. It fell on them from above at up to 80 kilometres an hour, gripped the hips with one
  foot and struck the head or neck with the other. That is why the one of the game dives.
- **A small eagle that grew.** Its DNA says that its nearest relatives are two of the smallest eagles in the
  world. In New Zealand there were no big mammal hunters, so an eagle took the place of the tiger, and it
  grew more than ten times heavier.
- **Why it died out.** The first people reached New Zealand about 750 years ago. They hunted the moa until
  none were left, and they burnt much of the forest. Without its prey, the eagle died out too, within a
  couple of centuries.
- **A legend.** The Maori tell of the pouakai, a giant bird that could carry off a person. It may be the
  memory of this eagle.
- **Nobody knows its colour.** Only its bones are known: the colours of the game are those of forest eagles
  of today.
