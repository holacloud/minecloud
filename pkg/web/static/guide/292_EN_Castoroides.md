# Castoroides

![Castoroides](mob:castoroides)

The **giant beaver**: a beaver **the size of a bear**, with a high round back, a flat tail and **two orange
front teeth as long as a hand**. It is an ancient relative of the [beaver](137), and it is peaceful. It is an
[extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has to be brought back to life.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was a
   Castoroides, you take ![](item:fossil_castoroides) `fossil_castoroides` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived Castoroides: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back.

## Behaviour

- **It walks slowly** near its place, low and heavy, and wags its flat tail.
- It looks around and flicks its small ears.
- **It builds nothing.** It fells no trees and makes no dams or lodges: that is the work of the [beaver](137)
  (see [Beaver dams](138)). Scientists think the real one did not build either.
- It does not dive or slap the water as the beaver does.
- It is **peaceful**: it comes up to nobody and attacks nobody. If you hit it, it gives a start.

It is **2.1 blocks** long and a little over 1 block tall: a beaver does not reach half its height. There is one colour
only: dark brown, paler under the belly, with a black scaly tail.

## Tips

- It does not run away and it does not bite: it is a good first animal to revive.
- It stays near the place where it was revived: build the machine where you want it to live. A pond next to
  it makes a good home, even if it does not need one.
- Put it next to a beaver to see the difference. In the [Bestiary](5), the sheet of both shows them together
  under the title **Evolution**.

## The real Castoroides

- **When and where.** It lived in **North America**, from Florida to Alaska, from about **3 million** to
  **10,000 years ago**, in lakes, ponds and swamps.
- **Its name** means "like a beaver".
- **Size.** It was between **1.9 and 2.2 metres** long and weighed around **100 kilos**: as much as a black
  bear. A beaver of today weighs about 20.
- **The teeth.** Its incisors were up to **15 centimetres** long. A beaver's are sharp as a chisel, for
  cutting wood; the giant's were blunter and grooved.
- **What it ate.** Scientists studied what its bones are made of, and it tells what an animal ate: **water
  plants**, not trees. So it seems it felled no trees and built no dams. It lived in the swamps that were
  already there.
- **The tail.** A tail has no bones to its tip: nobody knows its exact shape. It was probably longer and
  narrower than a beaver's.
- **Why it died out.** When the Ice Age ended the climate grew warmer and drier, and many of the swamps it
  needed dried up. The beaver, which **makes its own ponds** with its dams, is still here.
- **Is it the beaver's grandfather?** No. Both lived at the same time, side by side: Castoroides was of another
  branch of the beaver family, which left no descendants. A giant cousin.
