# Onychonycteris

![Onychonycteris](mob:onychonycteris)

One of the **first bats**: it flew already, but it had **a claw on every finger** of its wings, and it probably
could not yet find its way by echo. It is an ancient relative of the [bat](38). It is an
[extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has to be brought back to life.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was an
   Onychonycteris, you take ![](item:fossil_onychonycteris) `fossil_onychonycteris` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived Onychonycteris: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back.

## Behaviour

- **It flutters low**, near its place: 1.4 blocks over the ground, about as high as your eyes. It goes no
  higher.
- **It lands.** When it is going nowhere, it beats its wings on the spot for 1.5 seconds, comes down to the
  ground, **folds its wings** and stays there, propped on its claws. To go somewhere else, it takes off.
- It does not hang from roofs or sleep by day, as the bats of today do: you will see it by day and by night.
- It makes no clicks: it does not use echo.
- It is **peaceful**: it attacks nobody. If you hit it, it takes off, and it is a while before it lands again.

There are **2 colours**: brown and dark grey.

## Tips

- **To see its claws, wait for it to land** and come up slowly: it does not flee. They are the pale tips of
  its wings, five on each.
- While it is in the air it is right in front of your face: the best moment to watch how it beats its wings.
- At night, compare it with a bat of today: today's has bigger ears and longer wings, and never keeps still.
- In the [Bestiary](5), its sheet and the bat's show the two together under the title **Evolution**.

## The real Onychonycteris

- **When and where.** It lived about **52 million years ago** in what is now Wyoming, in **North America**.
  There were great lakes there then, with forests around them, and it was much warmer than today. Its fossils
  are two nearly whole skeletons that were left in the mud at the bottom of a lake. They were found in 2003,
  and it was given its name in **2008**.
- **Its name** comes from Greek and means "clawed bat".
- **The claws.** A bat of today has a claw on its thumb only; flying foxes, on the thumb and one more finger.
  The Onychonycteris had them **on all five fingers**: it got them from its ancestors, which climbed trees and
  did not fly yet.
- **Size.** It measured about **30 centimetres** from wingtip to wingtip and weighed about 40 grams: as much
  as a golf ball. In the game it is twice as big, so that its claws can be seen.
- **How it flew.** It had short, broad wings, and longer hind legs than a bat of today. Scientists think it
  flew by **flapping for a while and gliding for a while**, and that it was very good at climbing along
  branches. That is why the one of the game flies low and lands so often.
- **And the echo?** The bats of today that hunt insects shout and listen to the echo of their own voice to
  "see" in the dark. That takes a very big inner ear, and the inner ear of the Onychonycteris was small. So,
  most likely, **bats learnt to fly first and to use echo afterwards**: a question that had gone unanswered
  for many years. Not every scientist agrees: the fossil is squashed as flat as a leaf, and it is hard to be
  sure.
- **What it ate.** Insects: its teeth say so.
- **Is it the first bat?** It is the most primitive of those known from a whole skeleton. But it was a real
  bat already, with wings: the ones that were halfway there have not been found yet.
- **Is it the grandfather of the bats of today?** Not quite: it belongs to a branch that split very early
  from the tree of bats and left no descendants. A very old great-uncle rather than a grandfather.
- **Nobody knows its colour.** Fossils keep bones and teeth, not hair: the colours of the game are those of a
  bat of today.
