# How to find metals

![Copper ore](item:copper_ore)

Metals are not hidden at random. Each has its own lands and its own depth, and many **can be seen from outside**, if you know how to look. This page says where to search. The [next one](249) says how to get the metal out.

## Two ways to find them

- **Outcrops.** Patches of ore **in the open air**, a few blocks across. They show mostly on the bare rock of the **mountains**, and now and then on a **very steep slope** anywhere else. You find them by walking and looking.
- **Veins.** Groups of joined blocks, inside the stone. When you find one block, **keep digging around it**: there is almost certainly more. You find veins by digging, with nothing to see from above. Start where you already know there is something.

There are no caves to help you: you climb to look, or you dig to reach.

Silver and zinc also turn up **inside lead veins**, in small pockets. If you find lead, look closely around it.

## At what depth

Check your height, the **y**. The water is at y = 5 and the bottom of the world at **y = −14**. The further down you go, the less coal and the more metal.

| | In plains, forest and desert | In the mountains |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | mostly from y = 0 to y = 3, and some above and below | at any height, and more the higher you go |
| Iron | below y = 4 | at any height |
| Copper | below y = 4 | at any height |
| Tin | – | at any height |
| Zinc and lead | below y = 4 | at any height |
| Silver | very little, below y = 4 | little, at any height |
| Gold | very little, below y = 0, and **more the deeper you go** | little, at any height; below y = 0, **more the deeper you go** |

A vein never starts right under the grass: it needs a few blocks of soil and stone above it.

## A plan to start with

1. **Coal first**, since it lights everything else. Look for a steep slope or walk to a mountain: **a couple of minutes** is usually enough. You can also dig in a forest or on the plains, down to y = 3.
2. **Iron**, which turns up in the same places as coal, though less of it.
3. **Copper** in the mountains, or by digging in the desert.
4. **Tin, zinc and lead** take a while longer in the mountains.
5. **Silver and gold** are truly rare. You can walk for many minutes without seeing any in the open, which is why every mountain is worth a look. Otherwise, dig deep inside them.

The metals you find are good at the [lab bench](117) too, copper, iron and gold included: [How metals are smelted](249) explains how.

A piece of the world you have already explored stays as it was, with its old ores and its bottom at y = −5. The new outcrops and veins, and the deeper rock, only appear in land **nobody has visited yet**.

## Why there, in the real world

The game simplifies a lot, but its rules take after the real thing:

- **Coal:** it comes from swamp plants that were buried, squeezed and heated for a very long time. That is why there is so much of it in the green lands here.
- **Iron:** it is spread very widely. It forms even in peat bogs, as "bog iron", though most of it comes today from very old rocks, laid down in bands.
- **Copper:** as they change with water and air near the surface, copper minerals turn into **malachite**, a strong green. A green stain on rock warns of copper below. In the Atacama Desert, in Chile, the extreme dryness preserves green copper minerals.
- **Tin:** cassiterite turns up mostly in quartz veins next to granite, so here it belongs to the mountains. It is heavy and resists weathering, and in reality it also collects in rivers. Not in this game.
- **Lead, zinc and silver:** galena is the main ore of lead, grey and shiny, and it breaks into cubes. It often comes with sphalerite, the main ore of zinc, and sometimes holds a little silver (up to 0.5 %). Most silver is taken from the ores of other metals, such as those of lead and zinc.
- **Gold:** it is found as metal, unmixed, in quartz veins and in river gravel, where water carries it.

> **Careful in real life.** **Lead is toxic**, and it builds up in the body. Stones with shiny minerals can hold lead: if you find one, do not lick it, and wash your hands. And smelting metals at home is dangerous: here yes, there no.
