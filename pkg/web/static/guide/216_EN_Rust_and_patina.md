# Rust and patina

![Copper golem](mob:copper_golem)

Leave a shiny copper coin or an iron nail outdoors and, after a while, they no longer look new: the copper turns
brown and then green, and the iron gets covered in orange rust. Nobody painted them: **the air did**. This is
chemistry happening slowly, all around us. Your [copper golem](215) goes through it too.

## What is happening?

Metals like copper ([Cu](165)) and iron ([Fe](162)) like to **join with oxygen** from the air. The metal atoms give
away some of their electrons to the oxygen, and a new substance forms on the surface: an **oxide**. Chemists call
this **oxidation**, and when it slowly eats a metal, **corrosion**.

Water helps a lot: it carries the oxygen and dissolved substances to the metal. That is why things rust faster in the
rain, by the sea or in a wet cellar.

## Copper: from orange to green

1. **Shiny orange**: clean copper.
2. **Brown** (days or weeks): a very thin layer of **copper oxide** forms.

   2 Cu + O₂ → 2 CuO

3. **Green** (years): with water and the **carbon dioxide** (CO₂) of the air, the oxide becomes **copper carbonate**,
   a pale green crust called **patina** or **verdigris**.

   2 Cu + O₂ + CO₂ + H₂O → Cu₂(OH)₂CO₃

The great thing about patina: it **sticks** to the copper and **protects** it. Under the green layer the copper stays
copper for centuries. That is why old copper roofs and domes are green and still there.

> The **Statue of Liberty** in New York is covered in copper sheets thinner than two coins. When it was finished, in
> 1886, it was orange-brown like a new penny. In about 30 years it had turned completely green.

## Iron: rust

Iron needs **oxygen and water at the same time**. Together they make **rust**, a hydrated iron oxide, reddish brown:

4 Fe + 3 O₂ + water → 2 Fe₂O₃ · (water)

Unlike patina, rust is **crumbly and full of holes**: it flakes off and lets more air and water in, so the iron keeps
rusting until it is eaten away. Salt makes it even faster (cars rust more where roads are salted in winter, and ships
in sea water).

## How to stop it

| Method | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Wax, oil or paint** | A layer keeps air and water away from the metal | Your golem with honeycomb, painted bridges, an oiled bike chain |
| **Galvanising** | Iron coated with **zinc** ([Zn](166)): the zinc corrodes first and protects the iron | Buckets, fences, street lamps |
| **Stainless steel** | Iron mixed with chromium, which forms an invisible protective layer | Spoons, sinks |
| **Keeping it dry** | No water, no rust | Tools stored in a dry place |

## Try it at home (with an adult)

- **The three nails**: put one iron nail in a dry jar, one in water covered with a layer of oil (no air gets in) and
  one half in plain water. After a week, only the last one is really rusty: it needed **air and water**.
- **Clean a dull copper coin**: rub it with a little vinegar and salt (or ketchup!). The acid dissolves the brown
  oxide and the coin shines again. Rinse it and leave it out: in a few days it starts to darken again.

## In the game

- The [copper golem](215) goes from orange to brown to green over game days, faster in the rain or in water.
- **Honeycomb** waxes it (the wax method in the table) and stops the patina; an **axe** scrapes it back, like the
  vinegar on the coin.
