# Cryptobiosis

![A tun, in its tube](item:tardigrade_tun)

**Cryptobiosis** means "hidden life". It is when an animal shuts down so completely that it almost stops being alive, and starts again later. The [tardigrade](240) is the champion at it.

## The experiment, on a slide

1. Select a sample with tardigrades (or a `tardigrade_tube`) and right click a [microscope](238).
2. **Wait.** A slide dries out in one game day, 240 seconds. The panel tells you how long is left. You can close it with Escape: the slide stays on, drying, and another right click shows it again.
3. Near the end the tardigrades pull in their legs and shrink into **tuns**, little wrinkled barrels. Everything else stops.
4. Dry: look around. The paramecia and the rest are dead. Rotifers, amoebas and euglenas have shut themselves in **cysts**. Only the tun can come back in this game.
5. Press the **Drop** icon (this uses a `water_flask`, and you keep the bottle).
6. In about **20 seconds** the tun swells, its legs come out, and it walks away.

Everything else stays as it is. This is the point: after the drought, the tardigrade has the slide to itself.

You can also put a `tardigrade_tun` straight on a slide: it starts dry, and you can watch a drop of water bring it back. And drawing one off a dry slide gives you a tun. An oven, though, is no use: unlike frozen fish, a tun does not thaw in the fire.

## How it really works

When a tardigrade starts to dry out it does not just wither. It gets ready:

- It pulls in its head and legs, which makes it less exposed and slows the loss of water.
- It makes **special proteins** which, as the water goes, set like a kind of **glass** around the insides of its cells, holding everything in place. Some kinds also use a sugar called trehalose. When the water returns, the glass dissolves.
- It loses nearly all its water, and its metabolism drops to less than a hundredth of a percent of normal, too little to measure easily.

## What tuns have survived

- **Cold:** close to absolute zero, in the laboratory. And some tardigrades frozen in Antarctic moss for **30 years** woke up and went on to lay eggs.
- **Vacuum:** in 2007 tuns were flown outside a spacecraft in orbit. Many survived the vacuum of space, and a few even the full ultraviolet of the Sun.
- **Radiation:** doses about a thousand times what would kill a person. One species has a protein that seems to shield its DNA.
- **Heat:** a few minutes at well over 100 °C, in old experiments.

## Not quite superheroes

- These records are for tuns, in laboratories, and many die in the process. Nobody has shown they can live in space: they only endured it, without eating or moving.
- **Awake, a tardigrade is delicate.** Warm water for a day can kill it. It needs a film of water to live in at all.
- **Slow drying matters.** Dried too fast, it dies before it has time to prepare.
- They cannot wait for ever. Years, perhaps some decades: old stories of 120 years are doubtful.

## Science and game

**True:** the tun, drying out, the rotifers and other organisms that make cysts, and that most of the rest die.

**Simplified in the game:**

- A real slide dries in minutes, usually too quickly for the animal to prepare. Real tardigrades do this in moss that dries slowly, over hours.
- Waking up takes minutes or hours, not 20 seconds.
- Drying and freezing are different things: a frozen tardigrade does not need to be a tun. The game gives them one form, for simplicity.
- Real bdelloid rotifers dry out and come back very well too. In the game only the tardigrade does, to make the lesson clear.

**Why does it matter?** Scientists study these proteins to learn to keep vaccines and medicines without a fridge, which would be of great help in hot places far from anywhere.
