# Titanotylopus

![Titanotylopus](mob:titanotylopus)

A **giant camel**: long legs, a long neck, one tall hump and a shaggy dark coat. It stands **3.4 blocks**
tall, half as tall again as a [camel](29), of which it is an ancient relative. It is peaceful, and once tamed
**you can ride it**. It is an [extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has to be brought back to
life.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was a
   Titanotylopus, you take ![](item:fossil_titanotylopus) `fossil_titanotylopus` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived Titanotylopus: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back.

## Behaviour

- **It strolls** near its place with long, slow strides.
- It is a grazer: when it stands still, **it lowers its long neck to eat**.
- It turns its neck to look around and flicks its ears.
- **It lies down** as a camel does: it folds its legs under its body and rests on the ground. A tamed one does
  it when you tell it to stay. One that came out of an egg lies down by itself now and then, for 10 to 25
  seconds; a revived one that nobody has tamed does not: the server walks it.
- It is **peaceful**: it comes up to nobody and attacks nobody. If you hit it, it gives a start.

There is one colour only: reddish brown, with dark shaggy hair on the hump, the top of the legs, the head and
down the front of the neck.

## How to interact

| What you do | What happens |
|---|---|
| Hit it | **30 health**: 8 hits bare-handed, 5 with a stone or an iron [sword](71), 4 with a golden one. |
| Defeat it | It leaves **2 to 4** ![leather](item:leather) `leather` and **1 or 2** ![fur](item:fur) `fur`. |
| Tame it | Yes, with what tames a camel: `cactus`, `tall_grass` or `wheat`, with **1 in 3** per try. Then it follows you. See [Taming and breeding](3). |
| Ride it | **Right click** on a tamed one, with an empty hand or any block that is not its food. **Shift** gets off. A revived one has to be tamed first: only its owner rides it. See [Riding](7). |
| Make it lie down | **Shift + right click** (crouching) on a tamed one; the same again and it follows you. |
| Breed | Two tamed ones in love have a calf, which is too young to ride until it grows up. |
| Bait | None. |
| Cage | It is too big for the cage. |
| Egg | `titanotylopus_egg` |
| Command | `/mob titanotylopus` |

## Riding it

| | Titanotylopus | [Horse](34) |
|---|---|---|
| Your eyes, above the ground | **3.5 blocks** | 2.3 blocks |
| Speed, blocks per second | **7.5** | 9 |
| Galloping (double tap **W**) | **9.4** | 11.25 |
| Jump (**Space**) | about **2 blocks** | about 2.7 blocks |

- You sit in front of the hump, higher than on any horse: you see over walls and fences.
- It walks up 1-block steps by itself, as a horse does.
- **It needs 4 free blocks of height**: under a low roof or the branches of a tree you cannot get on, and
  riding into them stops you.
- Other players see you riding it.

## Tips

- `tall_grass` is everywhere: take a good handful and offer it one at a time until it stays with you.
- Do not carry its food in your hand when you want to get on: with `cactus`, `tall_grass` or `wheat` the
  right click feeds it.
- It is slower than a horse but you see much further from up there: good for exploring the desert and the
  plains, bad for the forest.
- Put it next to a camel to see the difference. In the [Bestiary](5), the sheet of both shows them together
  under the title **Evolution**.

## The real Titanotylopus

- **When and where.** It lived in **North America** from about **10 million** to about **300,000 years ago**.
- **Its name** means "giant padded foot". Camels do not walk on hoofs, as horses do, but on broad soft pads,
  which do not sink in sand or snow.
- **Size.** It stood about **3.5 metres** tall, and some scientists think it weighed more than **2,000
  kilos**: several times a camel of today. It had long, strong legs and big canine teeth.
- **The hump.** A hump is fat and hair: it leaves no fossil. But the bones of its back have tall spines, as
  those of a camel do, so scientists think it carried one. And a hump holds **fat, not water**: it is a
  larder for the days with nothing to eat.
- **What it ate.** Leaves, bushes and grass.
- **Camels are American.** The first camels, no bigger than a hare, lived in **North America** some **45
  million years ago**, and for most of their history camels lived nowhere else. About **7 million years ago**
  some crossed to Asia over the land that then joined Alaska and Siberia: from them come the camels of today.
  Others went down to South America about **3 million years ago**: from them come the llama and the alpaca.
- **Why it died out.** Nobody knows for sure: the climate changed many times while it lived. The last camels
  of North America disappeared about **10,000 years ago**, at the end of the Ice Age, when people had
  already arrived.
- **Is it the camel's grandfather?** Not quite: it belonged to a branch of giant camels that stayed in America
  and left no descendants. A great-uncle rather than a grandfather.
