# Glow block

![Glow block](item:glow_block)

A cube that shines by itself, in the colour you choose. It takes no power and no fuel. It is not realistic: nothing real glows for ever on nothing. It is there because it looks good.

| | In-game name | Id |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:glow_block) | Glow Block | `glow_block` |

## How to get it

| Recipe (C key) | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| 1 `glow_block` | 1 `glass` + 1 `torch` |

## Choosing its colour

**Right click** the block to open its panel (crouch to build against it). The block changes for everyone as you choose.

- **Colour temperature:** five whites, from a warm **2700 K**, like an old filament bulb, to a cold **6500 K**, like daylight in a clouded sky. It starts at 3000 K.
- **Colour:** red, green and blue sliders. Each has **6 steps**, 216 colours in all: a slider jumps to the nearest step, and the panel shows the colour you really get.

## What it does not do

- **It lights nothing around it.** It looks bright, in every graphics mode, but a dark room stays dark, and neither mobs nor [wheat](107) notice. For light, use a [torch](62), a [lamp or a lantern](228) or a powered [bulb](187).
- **It does not light a [microscope](238).**
- **It uses no [electricity](200)**, and carries none: wires do not join it.

Why not? The game's light has a strength and no colour. A red block giving out light would light everything around it white, which would look wrong.

## Breaking it

It breaks fast, and you get back a plain `glow_block`: **the colour is lost**, as your inventory only keeps how many of each thing you have. [Blueprints](191) do keep the colours. And a [motor ram](217) pushes it with its colour on.

## Science and game

A real thing that glows needs energy from somewhere. The **kelvin** is real, though: heat a piece of metal and it glows red, then orange, yellow and white, and the colour of a light is named after the temperature of a hot body of the same colour. That is why *warm* light has the lower number, the opposite of what you would guess: 2700 K is orange, 6500 K is bluish.

And a screen makes all its colours from just red, green and blue, as here. Yours has 256 steps per channel, which is more than 16 million colours.
