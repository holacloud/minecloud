# Volcanic palace

![Magma Rock](item:magma_rock)

On a land of black rock, ash and charred trunks, a crack in the ground glows orange at the bottom. A bridge crosses it to a gatehouse, and beyond, a wall of six sides bristling with **columns of basalt** surrounds a dark citadel that climbs a crag of rock, crowned by a tower with pinnacles. A sign by the bridge gives its name: the **Palace of the Volcano**: basalt, obsidian and magma rock, and inside, the **hall of minerals**.

## What inspired it

This palace is not modelled on a real one: it is a fantasy, built from what a volcano gives. But its rocks and shapes are real:

- Its wall and its towers are bundles of **basalt columns**, like those of the **Giant's Causeway** in Northern Ireland, **Fingal's Cave** in Scotland, or **Los Órganos** on the island of La Gomera, in the Canaries. Lava that cools slowly cracks into columns, most of them with **six sides**.
- It is built of ![](item:basalt) **basalt**, ![](item:obsidian) **obsidian**, ![](item:black_brick) dark brick and ![](item:slate) slate, with ![](item:magma_rock) **magma rock** glowing in channels, hearths and pools.
- Its **hot springs** recall those of Iceland, Japan or Yellowstone, where the heat of the Earth warms water that comes up from below.

## A walk through it

**The chasm and the bridge.** Across the front runs a chasm six blocks deep, with glowing magma rock along its bottom. A bridge of basalt with a path of ![](item:granite) granite and low walls of obsidian crosses it to the **gatehouse**: a passage under a raised portcullis, a **guard room** on each side and the gate chamber over it.

**The curtain wall** is a **hexagon**. Bundles of basalt columns of different heights hold it up like buttresses and form **bastions** at its six corners, the highest of each with a glowing cap.

**The ward**, inside the wall: paved in front, and behind, a garden of plants that grow on ash (moss, shrubs, grass, charred trunks), a vent that glows, a **hot spring** with columns to sit on, and crags of rough rock round the base the palace stands on.

**The palace**, on a podium four blocks high. Up the main stairs, between bundles of columns, a portico of six columns leads under a stepped gable with a **rose window of red glass**.

- **Ground floor, front:** the **great hall** (two long tables, a red carpet, **channels of magma** along the walls, chandeliers and banners); the **kitchen**, with four ovens under a hood and a **magma pit** to roast over; and the **dining room**, with a table for ten.
- **West wing:** the **hot spring baths** (two pools in a frame of warm rock, benches, towels) and the **hall of minerals** (below).
- **East wing:** the **treasury**, with the keeper's desk and, through an iron door, its vault; and the **forge and foundry**: a hearth of magma under its hood, two foundries, an oven, anvils of iron, a trough to cool the metal, and barrels of ore.
- **At the back, on the axis:** the **throne room**, two floors high. An ![](item:obsidian_throne) obsidian throne stands on a dais of red tile, and behind it rise columns like the pipes of an organ, highest in the middle, glowing and gilded at the top.
- **The crater courtyard**, in the middle of the four wings: a hexagonal court round a **sunken pool of magma** behind iron bars, with a plug of basalt columns rising from it, two **hot springs** and a colonnade whose roof is a terrace of the first floor.

**First floor:** the servants' room, the **library** (shelves on every wall, a long reading table, a globe and an astrolabe), the guest suite (bedroom, dressing room, antechamber and washroom), and a corridor in each wing with bedrooms and a study. The big bedrooms have a small washroom behind them (bathtub, basin and toilet) and a closet, and the servants have one of their own. Daily life is left on the tables: meals half eaten, open books, a letter half written, a game of chess; some curtains are drawn and others open.

**The roof:** terraces all round, with beds of ash soil, hardy plants, charred trunks and seats made of columns.

**The keep**, a tower of six sides standing on the roof of the throne room. A stair winds round a pillar inside it, past the antechamber, the **royal bedchamber**, the **observatory** and the watch room, up to a platform with a **crown of pinnacles**: the highest point of the palace.

## The science corner: the hall of minerals

Its floor is paved in **hexagons** of granite edged in obsidian, like the tops of basalt columns. Along one wall stand **fourteen glass cases**, each with a rock or an ore on a plinth of obsidian and its label; along another, seven cases of **elements**; on the third wall, the lessons.

### The three families of rocks

Every rock on Earth belongs to one of three families, depending on **how it was born**:

| Family | How it is born | In the cases |
|---|---|---|
| **Igneous** | lava or magma that cooled and hardened | ![](item:basalt) basalt, ![](item:granite) granite, ![](item:obsidian) obsidian, ![](item:magma_rock) magma rock |
| **Sedimentary** | sand, mud and shells that settled in layers and were pressed into rock by the weight above | ![](item:limestone) limestone, ![](item:sandstone) sandstone, coal (and sand, which in time becomes sandstone) |
| **Metamorphic** | a rock changed by heat and pressure, **without melting** | ![](item:marble) marble (once limestone), ![](item:slate) slate (once mud) |

**Fast or slow?** Igneous rocks show how fast they cooled. Granite cooled **slowly**, deep underground, and its crystals had time to grow big enough to see. Basalt cooled **fast**, at the surface, and its grain is very fine. Obsidian cooled **so fast** that no crystals formed at all: it is a natural **glass**.

The three families are linked in the **rock cycle**: a rock can be worn down into sand, pressed into a new rock, cooked into another, melted into magma and born again, over millions of years.

> **What is a fossil?** A living thing buried in mud. Over millions of years, minerals turn it to stone. That is why fossils are found in **sedimentary** rocks: lava would have burnt them.

### Basalt columns

In a corner stands a **model of basalt columns**. When a thick sheet of lava cools, it **shrinks** and cracks, as mud does when it dries in the sun. The cracks meet at angles that make **six-sided prisms**: the hexagon is the shape that splits a surface into equal pieces with the shortest cracks. The Giant's Causeway has about **40,000** of them, from lava that cooled some 60 million years ago.

### Ores and elements

The other cases show the **ores**, rocks with metal in them: iron, copper, tin and gold. "Gold hardly mixes with anything," says its label, "so it sits in the rock as pure metal." Tin and copper together make bronze. Seven more cases hold blocks of the **elements** that rocks are made of:

| | Element | Its label says |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:element_si) | Silicon | with oxygen it makes almost every rock of the crust |
| ![](item:element_fe) | Iron | the metal of iron ore, and of the centre of the Earth |
| ![](item:element_cu) | Copper | the metal of copper ore |
| ![](item:element_sn) | Tin | the metal of tin ore |
| ![](item:element_au) | Gold | the metal found already pure in the rock |
| ![](item:element_ag) | Silver | another precious metal of ores |
| ![](item:element_s) | Sulfur | it comes out yellow from volcanic vents, and smells of rotten eggs |

There are also three **chests of samples**, each labelled on its lid: one of ![](item:pyrite) pyrite ("fool's gold", iron and sulfur), ![](item:fluorite) fluorite and ![](item:meteoric_iron) iron from a meteorite; one of **fossils**, trilobites and ammonites of ancient seas, and ![](item:amber) amber, hardened tree resin; and one of **rocks to take**, one of each family. On a desk stands a ![](item:microscope) **microscope**, with glass slides, test tubes and thermometers in the chest beside it.

### Signs around the palace

- **Lava lake** (in the crater courtyard): lava is molten rock, hotter than 1,000 °C. As it cools it turns into basalt or obsidian.
- **Hot springs:** rain seeps down cracks, the hot rock of the volcano warms it, and it comes back up, with salts dissolved in it.
- **Forge and foundry:** metal is won from ore here: iron, copper, tin and gold.

## What the chests hold

- **Kitchen and dining room:** bread, cheese, fish fresh and salted, mushrooms, honey, wheat, salt, milk, cold water and soda water.
- **Hall of minerals:** pyrite, fluorite, meteoric iron, gold ore, fossils of trilobite and ammonite, amber, and samples of basalt, granite, obsidian, marble, limestone and sandstone.
- **Forge:** coal, iron, copper, tin and gold ores, sand, quicklime, brass and bronze ingots, iron and copper blocks, copper wire and a wrench.
- **Treasury:** gold ore, gold leaf and gold blocks, rubies, sapphires, amber and meteoric iron.
- **Library, study and observatory:** star charts, blueprints, breeding notebooks, a telescope, a barometer, a thermometer, an anemometer, a globe and a sundial.
- **Guard rooms, bedrooms and gardens:** torches, lanterns, bread, wool, furs, warm coats, saplings and fertilizer.

## Curiosities

- In **Iceland**, almost every home is heated with water warmed by volcanic rock, piped from underground.
- The legend of the Giant's Causeway says it was built by the giant **Finn McCool** to walk across the sea to Scotland, where the same columns appear again at Fingal's Cave. The real link is the lava: one great flow reached both places.
- Near Girona, in Catalonia, the village of **Castellfollit de la Roca** stands on top of a cliff of basalt columns 50 metres high.
- **Obsidian** was so prized for its sharp edges that people traded it hundreds of kilometres from the volcanoes it came from. The Maya made knives and mirrors with it (see the [Maya palace](319)).
- Most of the Earth's crust is made of just two elements, **oxygen** and **silicon**, joined in the minerals of almost every rock.
