# Lifeless things under the microscope

![Microscope](item:microscope)

Not everything worth looking at is alive. A shaving of wood, a pinch of sand, a crystal of salt, a feather, the board of a logic gate: put almost anything ordinary on a slide and it has something to show. Nothing on these slides moves or dries out, and there is nothing to identify or capture: each is looked at for itself. **Select it and right click a free [microscope](238)**, as with any sample: it uses one slide and one of the item.

Under the field, a line says what it is and what the power you are using shows. Change the power and the line changes with it. A [stain](246) changes it again: it says what the dye did, or why it did nothing.

## Some to start with

- **Oak and spruce side by side.** Oak has big vessels in a band at the start of each year's ring. Spruce has no vessels at all: a conifer is made of tracheids, in neat rows, with a few resin canals. A tropical wood from the jungle has hardly any rings: with no seasons, growth barely marks it.
- **Sand at 40×, then turn the focus.** The grains lie at different heights: the focus picks which is sharp.
- **Salt, then a drop of stain.** The water in the dye dissolves the cubes: their corners round off.
- **Wheat seeds with iodine.** The starch goes blue-black, big lens-shaped grains and small round ones: wheat has both. The row of aleurone cells under the bran stays brown, because it is protein.
- **A logic gate at 40×, then 400×.** Copper tracks and a black chip, then the glass-fibre cloth inside the board.
- **Gold leaf.** It is green, because gold beaten this thin lets green light through.
- **Meteoric iron at 40×.** Bands crossing at 60°: they grew as the iron cooled, very slowly, inside an asteroid.

## What the stains do here

| | Iodine | Methylene blue |
|---|---|---|
| Starch (wheat, bread, wood rays in sapwood, chloroplasts, some paper) | **blue-black** | nothing special |
| Living cells (seed, cactus, moss, grass, muscle) | yellow-brown | **nuclei dark blue** |
| Protein (wool, hair, feather, leather, scale) | yellow-brown | some blue |
| Minerals, metals, glass, gems, shell | nothing | nothing |
| Salts (salt, baking soda, copper sulphate) | they dissolve | they dissolve |

Why minerals, metals and glass stay as they were, and why metals go black at high power, is on [Opaque, clear and amorphous](259).

## Science and game

**True:**

- Each family looks the way it does in real microscopes: ring-porous oak, resin canals in spruce, rutile silk in corundum, green gold leaf, the Widmanstätten pattern, pleochroic halos in biotite, dumbbell-shaped silica bodies in grass, hooklets on barbules, the stripes of muscle.
- Real rock is looked at in **thin section**: a slice ground to 30 µm so that light passes.

**Simplified in the game:**

- A real slide of a block would need cutting, grinding or polishing first. Here any item goes straight on.
- A real ice slide would melt in minutes on a warm stage.
- Every metal here is shown already polished and etched, which takes a lab hours.
