# Periodic table

![Iron element block](item:element_fe)

The **periodic table** orders the chemical elements by their **atomic number** (how many protons each atom has in its nucleus). The **rows** are called **periods** and the **columns** **groups**: elements in the same group look alike and react in similar ways.

The [lab bench](117) draws the game's 36 elements in their place on the real table, coloured by family. Click one and its 📖 button opens its page here. Of the 118 known elements, the game has 36.

## The families

- **Alkali metals** (group 1, except hydrogen): soft, and so reactive that they are stored in oil.
- **Alkaline earth metals** (group 2): the calcium in your bones and in limestone.
- **Transition metals** (groups 3–12): iron, copper, gold, silver...
- **Other metals:** aluminium, tin and lead, softer and with lower melting points.
- **Metalloids:** boron and silicon, halfway between metal and nonmetal.
- **Nonmetals:** hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus and sulfur, the building blocks of life.
- **Halogens** (group 17): fluorine, chlorine and iodine; their name means "salt makers".
- **Noble gases** (group 18): helium, neon and argon, which almost never react.

## Trivia

- Dmitri Mendeleev published his table in **1869** and left gaps for elements nobody knew yet. He predicted what they would be like, and he was right when gallium and germanium were discovered.
- In groups 1, 2 and 13–18, the group tells how many electrons the atom has in its outer shell, and that decides what it bonds with: that is why sodium (group 1) and chlorine (group 17) fit together so well in salt.
- Element 101 is called **mendelevium** in his honour.
