# Castle

![Stone bricks](item:stone_bricks)

On open ground you may come across a **castle**: a square of high stone walls about **100 blocks** on each side, with a moat of water all round, nine towers flying flags, a courtyard full of life and, in the middle, a pale tower taller than everything else: the **keep**. It is inspired by the castles of the Middle Ages in Europe, but it is not a copy of any of them.

## A little real history

For about five hundred years, from the 10th to the 15th century, a castle was a home and a fortress at once: the lord lived in it with his family, his soldiers and his servants, and the people around ran to it when there was danger.

- **The first castles were of earth and wood.** The Normans raised a mound of earth (the *motte*) with a wooden tower on top and a yard closed by a fence (the *bailey*). They were quick to build: a few weeks.
- **Then came stone.** Wood burns, stone does not. Around 1078 William the Conqueror began the **White Tower** in London, a square keep of stone that still stands.
- **Walls inside walls.** In the 13th century the best castles had two rings of walls, one inside the other, like **Beaumaris** in Wales or **Krak des Chevaliers** in Syria: whoever broke through the first found himself trapped in front of the second.
- **Spain is the land of castles**: the kingdom of **Castile** takes its name from them, because its frontier was lined with them. The Alcázar of Segovia, on a rock between two rivers, and the brick castle of Coca are two famous ones.
- **Gunpowder ended them.** From the 15th century cannons could knock down any wall, and castles slowly turned into palaces, prisons or ruins.

In Spanish the keep is called *torre del homenaje*, "tower of homage", because it was where vassals knelt and swore loyalty to their lord.

## How to read the defences

| Part | What it is | What it was for |
|---|---|---|
| **Moat** | A ditch full of water round the walls | It stops ladders, siege towers and enemies digging tunnels under the walls |
| **Curtain wall** | The wall between two towers | To keep everyone out; a **wall walk** runs along its top |
| **Battlements** | The tooth-shaped top of a wall | The teeth (*merlons*) shelter the archer; the gaps (*crenels*) let him shoot |
| **Machicolations** | A parapet that sticks out on stone brackets (*corbels*) | Through the holes in its floor defenders dropped stones on whoever stood at the foot of the wall |
| **Arrow slits** | Tall, very narrow openings | Wide inside and narrow outside: the archer could aim, but was a tiny target |
| **Portcullis** | A grille that slides down in front of the gate | To close the way in a moment, even if the doors were broken |
| **Gatehouse** | Two towers guarding the gate | The gate is the weakest spot, so it got the most defence |

## A walk through the castle

### Bridge and gate

A **road** leads to the only way in: a **wooden bridge** over the moat, on stone piers, with rails and torches. The moat is three blocks deep, with banks and bed of stone. At the end stands the **gatehouse** between two towers with blue flags: two pairs of dark oak doors and, above them, the **portcullis** of ![](item:iron_bars) iron bars, drawn up. Over the passage there is a room, and on top machicolations, battlements and a red and yellow banner. With the doors shut nobody walks in, and the moat stops whoever tries to swim round.

### The walls and the towers

The **curtain walls** are thick: inside, at the foot, runs a **gallery** with arrow slits closed by bars; above, two skins of stone; on top, the **wall walk** with its parapet on corbels and stepped buttresses outside. The stone is a mix of ![](item:stone_bricks) stone bricks, stone and cobblestone, as old walls are, mended over the centuries, on a dark plinth of ![](item:basalt) basalt.

There are **nine towers**, all **octagonal**: four at the corners, with a flat top behind a parapet (red and blue flags), three in the middle of the sides with a **cone of** ![](item:slate) **slate**, a gold tip and a yellow flag, and the two of the gate. Each has five floors, with arrow slits and ![](item:lantern) lanterns, and a **spiral stair** that winds round a stone pillar in the middle. Doors on the courtyard, on the gallery and on the wall walk join everything: you can walk all the way round the castle along the top of its walls.

### The courtyard

- **Garden and well**, on the left of the gate: flower beds, small trees, benches, and in the middle a stone **well** with a wooden roof and deep [water](52). In a siege, a castle without a well was lost in days.
- **The market**, on the right: six stalls under striped awnings, with flowers, mushrooms, vases and pots on the counters and a barrel behind each.
- **The stables**: a hall of ![](item:brick) brick and timber with stalls behind fence gates, hay and a barrel in each.
- **The forge**: stone below, brick above and a tall chimney; three ![](item:oven) ovens are its fires, beside a workbench, chests and barrels.

Small signs on posts name every place in Spanish and English.

### The keep

The keep is of ![](item:limestone) limestone on a dark plinth, with walls two blocks thick, and you go in up some steps, through a doorway framed in ![](item:marble) marble. Inside, a spiral stair in one corner goes from the dungeon to the roof.

| Floor | What there is |
|---|---|
| **Dungeon** (underground) | A corridor with **eight cells**, each behind a wall of bars and an ![](item:iron_door) iron door |
| **Ground floor** | The **great hall**: a floor of marble in a check, a red carpet, two long tables with chairs and ![](item:candle) candles, columns with red and yellow hangings, ![](item:chandelier) chandeliers on chains and, at the end, a **throne** with a gold back on a stepped dais. Behind it, the **kitchen**: a row of ovens under a brick hood, a long table, stools, a sink, barrels and chests |
| **Bedrooms** | A corridor with a blue carpet and **seven bedrooms**, each in its own colour, with a ![](item:bed) bed, a bedside table and lamp, wardrobes and chests. At the back, the **royal chamber**: two beds, purple curtains, a desk and paintings |
| **Library and armoury** | Rows of ![](item:bookshelf) bookshelves and reading desks; behind an iron door, the **armoury**, with chests, barrels, wall shelves and racks of iron |
| **Chapel** | A tall room with tall windows of [stained glass](229) in six colours and a round **rose window** at each end, benches, columns and chandeliers |
| **Roof** | A walk behind battlements on corbels, a **turret** with a slate roof at each corner, and the slate roof of the chapel with a gold finial and a purple flag |

## Light without electricity

A medieval castle had no electricity, and neither has this one: at night it is lit by ![](item:lantern) lanterns, ![](item:candle) candles, torches, floor and table lamps and chandeliers. All of them are [lights without electricity](228). Look at how a hanging lantern lights a whole floor of a tower.

## What the chests hold

Every chest, barrel and wardrobe of the castle holds what its room needs. Each market barrel has a trade of its own: bread and cheese, salted fish, honey and candles, wool, pots and jugs, mushrooms. The stables keep wheat for the horses, the forge coal and ore in its barrels and iron tools and ingots in its chests, the kitchen wheat, salt, cheese, honey and the plates and jugs for the hall. In the bedrooms there are warm coats, wool, leather and candles, and in the royal chamber furs, gold leaf and a few gems. The armoury holds iron swords, shields and scale mail, with torches and rations in its barrels, and the dungeon's barrel bread and buckets for the prisoners. Two chests side by side rarely hold quite the same.

## The science corner

- **Why octagonal towers?** A square corner is easy to knock down and hides a blind spot that archers cannot see. A round or many-sided tower deflects stones and lets defenders see all round it.
- **Why a spiral stair?** It takes very little room and can be defended step by step. Many say they turned so that a right-handed defender coming down could swing his sword freely; historians argue about it, because plenty of stairs turn the other way.
- **Water that stays.** In this game water does not flow away, so the moat and the well stay full without an outlet.
- **Stone and lime.** Real castles were held together by lime mortar, made by burning limestone: see [Quicklime](183).

## Curiosities

- Some castles were built in just a few years; others took a century and were never finished.
- The walls of many keeps were thicker than a grown-up is tall, which kept them cool in summer and warm in winter.
- The towers at the corners give archers a view along the whole wall: whoever climbed it was seen from the side.
