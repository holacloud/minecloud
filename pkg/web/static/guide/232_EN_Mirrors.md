## How to use them

- A **wall mirror** attaches to the **wall you click**, its silver looking out. It does not go on floors or ceilings, and it stays where it is if you break the wall, like a [torch](62) or a [sign](64).
- A **mirror cube** is a whole block with silver on all six sides.
- Stand close and **look at yourself**: the nearest mirror you can see, and all the mirrors in line with it, show the world and you. Farther than about 14 blocks, or on another wall, a mirror just looks like shiny silver.
- Looking around in a mirror asks your computer to draw the world twice, so only one wall of mirrors at a time reflects.

## How does a mirror work?

Light travels in straight lines and **bounces** off things. Most things are rough, so light bounces off them in all directions: you can see the thing, but no picture of what lit it.

A mirror is so **smooth** that every ray bounces the same way, like a ball off a wall: it leaves at the same angle at which it arrived. Your eyes follow the ray straight back, so what you see seems to be **behind** the glass, as far behind as it really is in front.

Try this: raise your right hand. Your image raises the hand on the same side. A mirror does not swap left and right, it swaps **front and back**.

## Why silver?

Metals have electrons that move freely, and those electrons send light back. [Silver](167) reflects more visible light than any other metal. The glass only keeps it flat and protects it from the air, which would darken it. Page [181](181) tells the reaction that lays it on the glass.
