# Breeding notebook

![Breeding notebook](item:breeding_notebook)

A notebook with wooden covers where a breeder writes down the **genes** of their animals: what each one carries,
who its parents were and what its babies could look like. It is the tool for [Genetics](130).

| | In-game name | Id |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:breeding_notebook) | Breeding Notebook | `breeding_notebook` |

## How to get it

| Recipe (C key) | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| 1 `breeding_notebook` | 2 `planks` + 1 `feather` |

The [feather](75) is the quill; birds drop them when you tap them.

## How to use it

Hold the notebook and **right click a tamed animal** ([Taming and breeding](3)). A page opens with:

- **Its name and number:** every tamed animal gets a name (Luna, Bruno, Mendel...) so you can tell them apart.
- **Its genes:** the genotype of each gene (such as `Ww`, `Ll`, `Nf`), its alleles and its phenotype. "Carries
  brown" means it hides a brown allele.
- **Its family tree:** its parents and grandparents, if they were born in your care. Animals tamed in the wild
  have unknown parents.
- **A Punnett square:** right click a **second tamed animal of the same species** right after the first one, and
  the page shows the square of their coat colours plus the odds of every colour, size and speed among their babies.

Close the page with **Escape**, the Close button or a click outside it.

## Good to know

- It does not get used up, and it does not change the animal: the click does not feed it or make it sit.
- On a wild animal it only tells you to tame it first: the notebook keeps records of your own animals.
- The odds are for one baby. With four babies you will not always get exactly three white and one brown, just as
  four coin tosses do not always give two heads.
- Mutations (2 %) are not in the odds: they are surprises.

## Tips

- **Find the carriers:** read all your white goats. Pair the ones marked `Ww` and you will soon have brown kids.
- **Fast horses:** keep the foals marked `ff` or `fs` (fast) and breed them together.
- **Albinos:** when a baby is born "unusual", read it. If it carries `a`, breed it with its parent and use the
  Punnett square to see the chance.

## Trivia

- Real breeders have kept **herd books** for centuries, with the parents of every horse, cow or dog. The first
  thoroughbred horse stud book was published in England in 1791.
- Gregor Mendel kept careful notebooks with the counts of thousands of pea plants: that is how he spotted the
  3 to 1 ratio.
