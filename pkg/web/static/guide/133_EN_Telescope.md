# Telescope

![Telescope](item:telescope)

A copper tube with two glass lenses. It brings far things closer: the stars at night and the landscape by day. And
it tells you **what you are looking at**.

| | In-game name | Id |
|---|---|---|
| ![](item:telescope) | Telescope | `telescope` |

## How to get it

| Recipe (C key) | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| 1 `telescope` (*Craft Telescope*) | 2 `glass` + 1 `copper_block` + 2 `planks` |
| 1 `telescope` (*Craft Telescope (Iron)*) | 2 `glass` + 2 `iron_ore` + 2 `planks` |

Glass is made from sand: see [Sand, cactus and glass](51). The copper block is in
[Fine stones and metals](59). Iron is in [Ores](53).

## How to use it

1. Hold it and **right click**: you raise it to your eye. The view **zooms in smoothly** (about **6 times**) and
   the edges darken, like a real telescope.
2. Aim at something. A card at the bottom shows **its name**, **what it is** and a fun fact, plus that night's
   **moon phase**.
3. **Right click** again to lower it. It also lowers by itself if you switch items, open a menu or go to third
   person.

While you look through it:

- The mouse moves **slower**, so you can aim precisely.
- **Many more stars** come out than with the naked eye.
- **Faint lines** join the stars of each constellation, and a **label** names the one in front of you.

## What you can look at

| Target | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| A **constellation** | its name and how many stars it has |
| A **named star** | its name and which constellation it belongs to |
| A **planet** (Ferro, Aurum) | how long it takes to go round the sky |
| The **moon** | tonight's phase, or the eclipse if it is red |
| The **comet** | that it is only here tonight |

All of it is explained in [Astronomy](132).

## Good to know

- **Do not look at the sun.** Aim the telescope at the sun and you pull it away from your eye with an "Ouch!".
- By day there are no stars: the card tells you to wait for the night. But it works as a **spyglass** to explore
  the landscape.
- If you carry a [star chart](134), watching a constellation for a moment **records it**.
- They say whoever watches a **rare event** through the telescope reads something written in the sky...

## Trivia

- Galileo pointed a telescope at the sky in 1609 and saw mountains on the moon and four moons around Jupiter.
- The first telescopes magnified about 3 times; Galileo's reached 20.
- Today's big telescopes do not use lenses but **mirrors**, some wider than a house.
