# Giant argali

![Giant argali](mob:argali)

A wild sheep **as tall as a pony**, long in the leg, with **two horns that curl once around**. It is an ancient
relative of the [sheep](17). It minds its own business, but **if you hit it, it rams**. It is an
[extinct animal](261): it never appears by itself, it has to be brought back to life.

The argali is not gone altogether: it still lives in the mountains of Asia. The one of the game is the argali
of the Ice Age, the one that lived in Europe, and that one did die out. It is told below.

## How to get one

1. **Look for its fossil.** It lies far down, inside the stone, in the block ![](item:fossil_rock_rare)
   `fossil_rock_rare`: a dark rock with a three-toed footprint. The page on [fossils](262) says how deep to dig.
2. **Break the block** with a [pickaxe](68). Which animal is inside depends on the place: if it was a giant
   argali, you take ![](item:fossil_argali) `fossil_argali` with you.
3. **Bring it back to life** in the [revival machine](264). **One piece** is enough and it takes **45 seconds**. It is
   a land animal: it needs no water.

The server keeps a revived giant argali: every player sees it, and it is still there when you come back.

## Behaviour

- **It strolls** near its place, a little lighter on its feet than a sheep, and **grazes**: when it stands
  still, it lowers its neck to the grass.
- It is **neutral**: it attacks nobody who has not attacked it.
- **If you hit it, it rams.** It gives a deep bleat, turns on you, **lowers its horns** and stamps the ground
  with one leg for 0.8 seconds. Then **it runs in a straight line** at where you were: about 4 blocks, at 7
  blocks a second. If it gets within 1.6 blocks of you, the horns do **2 damage** and **throw you into the
  air**. After that it stands still for a little over a second.
- It only rams whoever hit it, and only that player sees the ram.
- It does not jump off cliffs or go through fences: when something is in its way, it stops.

There is one colour only: sandy brown, with a white belly, rump, muzzle and legs, and a ruff of white hair on
its neck.

## Tips

- **Step aside as soon as it lowers its horns.** It runs at where you were when it set off, not at where you
  are now: one long step to the side and it goes past.
- A fence between the two of you stops it.
- **The shove is worse than the blow**: it throws you further than a [goat](32) does. Do not hit it at the
  edge of a cliff.
- With a [shield](252) in your left hand, and facing it, the horns do half the damage.
- Put it next to a sheep to see the difference. In the [Bestiary](5), the sheet of both shows them together
  under the title **Evolution**.

## The real argali

- **It is still alive.** The argali is the biggest wild sheep in the world, and it **still lives** in the
  mountains and steppes of Central Asia, from the Altai to the Himalaya, sometimes more than 5000 metres up.
  There are fewer every year, mostly because of hunting.
- **The one of the game is the argali of the Ice Age.** Hundreds of thousands of years ago there were argalis
  in **Europe** too, from Portugal to Georgia. The best known come from a cave in the south of France, the
  Caune de l'Arago, and are about **450,000 years** old: the people who lived in it hunted them. That argali
  of Europe **did die out**, and nobody quite knows why.
- **Why "giant"?** Because among sheep it is one. A ram of today can stand **1.35 metres** at the shoulder
  and weigh more than **150 kilos**, and those of the Ice Age were as big as the biggest of today. They were
  no giants next to other argalis.
- **The horns.** A ram's horns grow in a spiral: the longest measure **1.9 metres** along the curve, and the
  two together can weigh more than **20 kilos**. They never fall off or stop growing, and every winter leaves
  a ring on them: count the rings and you know its age.
- **What they are for.** For rams to fight each other: they rear up on their hind legs and **crash** head
  against head. The crack is heard hundreds of metres away.
- **What it eats.** The tough grasses and low plants of the mountain and the steppe.
- **Hair, not wool.** Wild sheep have hair, with a thin coat of wool under it that they shed every spring.
  Long wool that never falls off by itself was the work of people, who chose which sheep to breed for
  thousands of years. That is why in the game the shears take `fur` from it, and not wool.
- **Is it the sheep's grandfather?** No. The farm sheep comes from the **mouflon** of Asia, which was tamed
  about **10,000 years** ago. The argali is its big cousin.
- **Its colour.** The game gives it the colour of the argalis of today: light brown, with a white rump and
  white legs, and a white ruff on the rams in winter. Of those of the Ice Age only bones are left.
