# Stained glass

![Blue stained glass](item:blue_stained_glass)

Glass in **16 colours**, as a full block and as a thin pane. Look through it and the whole world behind takes its colour, just like a real stained-glass window: the colour is a **filter**. Put a red pane behind a blue one and very little gets through, because each one holds back what the other lets pass.

## Why is glass coloured?

Plain glass is made from sand: silicon ([Si](152)) and oxygen ([O](146)). Nearly all light passes through it, so it looks clear.

For well over a thousand years, glassmakers have added a pinch of **metal oxide** to the melted glass. The atoms of the metal stay trapped in it and **eat certain colours of light**, and you see what is left. It only takes very little!

Red glass was the hardest. Glassmakers needed gold as fine as dust, in pieces far too small to see, and the recipe was lost for centuries. Those pieces are so small that they do not look gold at all: they swallow the green and let red through.

The great windows of medieval cathedrals were not painted over clear glass: their colours are in the glass itself, which is why they have not faded in 800 years.
